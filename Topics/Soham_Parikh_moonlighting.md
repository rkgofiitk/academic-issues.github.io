## Moonlighting isn't a Big Deal

Came across an interexting story of Soham Parekh. Soham claimed to have a Bachelor's degree from Bombay University and a masters from Georgia Institute of Technology. Mr. Suhail Dosh, CEO of an image generation startup, posted a [cautionary note about Soham's moonlighting to alert]( https://x.com/Suhail/status/1940294149955358882) potential employers. Soham came forward to tell his side of story. Apparently, he worked for 20 hours a day and seven days a week. None of the startup CEOs had any problem with Soham's technical abilities. A reliable source who as among people who had interviewed him tells me that he is a very competent software engineer, but had problem in meeting deadlines. The source also tells me that they realized Soham was unable to manage his time and deliver schedule. That is the reason for his firing after 4-5 months.  Soham's competence as an engineer is also a fact established by many employers who offered him remote jobs. However, Mr. Doshi's post raised two points:
- Is it ethically wrong to be a moonlighter if a person can manage to preserve NDAs and contact obligations?
- Whether the employer buy an exclusive right to employee's time, especially in a remote job contract?

The point of raising the above two issues comes from my earlier post about large scale [moonlighting during Covid 19 lockdown](./MoonShining.md). Many companies like Wipro, TCS, HCL, Infosys forced their employees to retrun back for physical presence in offices after the Covid scare receeded. Startups have a different work culture. Many expect founding engineers to work exclusively for the company without caring much of employee's time managment issues but expect competence. I know a lot of such instances when a startup explands too much before turning into a public company and announcment of IPOs. Many of these companies operate in India and sort of exploit field workers quite a lot. On the otherhand, there are startups who established a very sound policy of employee and employer relationships. I raised the issue of moonlighting with one of my students who established a cloud service startup in Bangalore a few years back. His company now employs more than 100 software engineers. He  is quite clear in his approach and says he does not care if an employee works for another startup so long as the employee is able to deliver preserving the project deadlines.
