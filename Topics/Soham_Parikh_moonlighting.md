## Moonlighting isn't a Big Deal

[Blog Index](../index.md)

An interesting case concerning moonlighting of one software engineer name Soham Parekh is all over the Internet. Mr. Suhail Doshi, CEO of an image generation startup, posted a [cautionary note about Soham's moonlighting to alert]( https://x.com/Suhail/status/1940294149955358882) potential employers. According to Mr. Doshi Soham was working as a remote tech employee for at least 3-4 US-based startups simultaneously. He accused Soham of pasrticularly targeting [YC startups](../YCombinator.md) for remote as a moonlighter. Many other startup CEOs were also allege that they have offered jobs to Soham. But it was unclear whether any of them suspect Soham of time slicing his working hours for moonlighting. Mr. Doshi's post was silent on two major issues as were the other CEOs who may have fired Soham in the past:
- Did anyone discover that Soham was working for several startups at the same time?
- Did Soham lack competence and unable to deliver the assigned tasks?

Even Mr. Doshi's post did not complain about the lack of competence though he casts doubt on Soham's educational background. Soham's LinkedIn profile says that he is with Dynamo AI. Interestingly, the company experises in AI delivering auditable AI gaurdrails, hallucination checks, red-beaming and observablity. 

Soham came forward to tell his side of story. Apparently, he worked for 20 hours a day and seven days a week. None of the startup CEOs of the companies which offered jobs to Soham had any issues with his technical competence. Many of them have interviewed him rigorously and offered jobs. A reliable source among the people who had interviewed him tells me that he is a competent software engineer. Many found that Soham had time management issues and evaded meetings requesting reschedulings. The source also tells me that they realized Soham was unable deliver as per promised schedule. That is the reason for his firing after 4-5 months. So, possibly no one could discover that Soham was holding concurrent responsibilities for several companies. In fact, many LinkedIn posts say that Soham might have worked for over 30 companies at a time. The numeric figure is unimportant, however, Mr. Doshi's post raised two points regarding practice and work ethics:
- Is it ethically wrong to be a moonlighter if a person can manage to preserve NDAs and contact obligations?
- Whether the employer buy an exclusive right to employee's time, especially in a remote job contract?

The point of raising the above two issues comes from my earlier post about large scale [moonlighting during Covid 19 lockdown](./MoonShining.md). Many companies like Wipro, TCS, HCL, Infosys forced their employees to retrun back for physical presence in offices after the Covid scare receeded. Startups have a different work culture. Many expect founding engineers to work exclusively for the company without caring much of employee's time managment issues but expect competence. I know a lot of such instances when a startup expands too much before turning into a public company and announcment of IPOs. Many of these companies operate in India and exploit field workers quite a lot. One specific US-based company that I know of works in area of fintech operating PoS devices and softwares.  On the otherhand, there are startups who established a very sound policy of employee and employer relationships. I raised the issue of moonlighting with one of my students who established a cloud service startup in Bangalore a few years back. His company now employs more than 100 software engineers. He  is quite clear in his approach and says that it hardly matters. It is acceptable, so long as the employee is able to deliver preserving the project deadlines and respects NDA compliances. However, established companies like Wipro or Infosys view employer-employee relationship in a traditional framework. They believe that a full-time employee is supposed to work only for their company all the time. Mr. Narayan Murthy, for example, wants an employee to slug for 70 hours a week for Infosys. Many other like Ravi Kumar of Cognizant wants employees work for 90 hours a week. Aren't they unethical exploitations? 

The media doesn't find much of an issue in exploitation, they mention exploitation as a casual statement without going into details like what is happening in the ground zero like the case of US-based fintech company mentioned above. Soham's case seems to have gone viral in mass and social media. However, according to Wall street Journal about 5.3% of skilled segment engage in moonlighting. It might have gone up a bit after Covid-19.
