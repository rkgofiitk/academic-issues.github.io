

 *India's Digital Sovereignty*

[Blog Index](../index.md)

 Executive Summary

India's digital infrastructure is vulnerable to suspension of cloud services by the US-based big tech companies like  Microsoft, Google, Meta, Oracle, SAP, and IBM. Recently, Indian refinery Nayara suddenly experienced a complete digital outage as Microsoft tried to enforce EU sanctions without advance notice against importing Russian crude. The incident is a wake-up call for India to fast-track the implementation of cloud services on indigenous home-grown platforms like Zoho, CtrlS, Meghdoot, NeuCloud, and encourage the development of a cloud workspace suite based on open source alternatives.


 1. Background and Strategic Context

Russia invaded Ukraine on February 24, 2022. The Russian invasion took place due to Ukraine's political ambitions for integration with NATO and the European Union. President Zelensky wanted to distance Ukraine from Russian influence. However, based on the 2001 census and regional data, approximately  30-35% of Ukraine's territory in the east and south is inhabited predominantly by the Russian-speaking people. Kyiv, Lviv, and Kharkiv are the three geographical regions controlling Ukraine's political and administrative power. People in the Russian speaking areas were unhappy with an oppressive Ukrainian state regime. The distribution of Russian-speaking areas is as follows. 

| Region/City |  % Ethnic Russians | Language Dominance|
|---|---|---|
|Sevastopol (Crimea)|	71.7%	|Predominantly Russian|
|Autonomous Republic of Crimea| 58.5% | Predominantly Russian|
|Donetsk Oblast| ~48%  | in cities like Donetsk, Makiivk |Strong Russian usage|
|Luhansk Oblast|	~50–60%| in several regions	|Strong Russian usage|


Overall, about 29.6% of Ukrainians identified Russian as their native language. 

On July 18, 2025, the European Union announced severe sanctions against countries dealing with Russia. Despite holding valid licenses, Microsoft unilaterally suspended cloud services to Nayara Energy without advance notice on July 24, 2025, citing the EU sanctions. Nayara is an Indian refinery with Russian oil major Rosneft having a 49% stake. The incident exposed the fragility of foreign tech dependencies. 

The Government of India (GoI) should consider the incidence of Microsoft's digital blocking of Nayara as a wake-up call. Many critical GoI operations depend on cloud and workspace suites offered by the US-based tech companies. These include revenue collection and other government operations, such as:

  * CBDT (Income Tax Business Application)
  * GSTN (Goods and Services Tax Network)
  * MCA21 (Corporate Affairs Portal)
  * UIDAI (Aadhaar infrastructure)

The primary responsibility of  the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) are the collection of: 
 
Personal income tax
Corporate tax
Securities transaction tax
CBDT net annual collection is approximately INR 222600 billion, which is 7.2% of India's GDP. 

The Goods and Services Tax Network (GSTN) is the authority responsible for collecting GST. Its net annual collection is approximately INR 203600 billion, 6.6% of GDP

MCA21 is the main corporate compliance and administration portal. It allows stakeholders -- companies, professionals, and citizens -- to access corporate data, statutory documents, and file compliance documents from anywhere. It enables MCA to monitor fraud, track compliance, and bring total transparency in the administration of corporate affairs.

UIDAI (Unified Identification Authority of India) is the statutory authority responsible for implementing and maintaining individual Aadhar biometric-based digital identification data. It recorded nearly 2.1 billion transactions for biometric verifications in May 2025. Access to all Government social service schemes is managed through Aadhaar verification system. 

All the critical system modules were implemented by Indian IT companies such as TCS, Infosys, HCL, and Wipro. However, they use various licensed products, such as Microsoft Azure Platform, Oracle, IBM enterprise products, and Google Workspace. Therefore, many critical functionalities may stop working or experience delays if dependence on foreign tech companies is not eliminated. Though it is unlikely that big tech companies will take the disastrous route of unilateral blocking of functionalities, the Nayara incident reveals a possibility.  

 2. Risk Assessment

To understand the real impacts, let us analyze the risks of using foreign tech products, especially in digital services. The use of digital systems transcends from education to the economy to modern warfare. Sindoor operation highlighted the capabilities of AI-activated India's air-defence and offensive systems of the IAF. It also underlined Indian IT's expertise in building integrated AI-assisted systems to meet the challenges of modern warfare. 

Indian fintech companies and startups have created a new finance and investment ecosystem. Remote education is now a reality and bringing world-class learning to the reach of poor students in remote villages in India. However, the government policy makers should be aware of dependencies on foreign products in Indian's digital space. They should plan to utilize the technical capabilities and expertise of Indian IT companies. A summary of the risk assessment is given in the table below. 

| Risk Type | Description | Impact Level | 
| --- | --- | --- | 
| License Breach | Foreign vendors can suspend services unilaterally | High | 
| Data Sovereignty | Sensitive citizen data stored in foreign clouds | High | 
| Compliance Overreach | Subject to U.S./EU sanctions and export controls | High | 
| Operational Paralysis | Disruption of tax filings, refunds, compliance | Critical | 


3. Strategic Objectives

India should work towards eliminating the influence of big tech companies in its digital ecosystem. Nayara-like incident is neither the first nor the last instance of denial of platform access to India.
Both Google and Meta removed Indian government or political content from YouTube and Facebook, citing global practices without legal mandates. Indian content creators were often blocked out, citing compliance with US or EU norms. 

Oracle licensing terms allow the US courts to exercise jurisdiction over Indian users. There are instances of Microsoft denying Azure Data localization, citing technical infeasibility. Indian defence and public sector establishments have experienced long delays in accessing specialized features and macros of MS Office. Therefore, India should plan for the following strategic goals:

Ensure uninterrupted access to critical government platforms
Preserve data sovereignty under Indian jurisdiction
Reduce/eliminate exposure to foreign sanctions and compliance regimes
Promote indigenous tech innovation and employment



4. Proposed Framework

India should evolve a framework of Indian Data Sovereignty around the following five pillars:
Audit and Assessments
Sovereign Tech Mandate 
Incentives for Migration 
Redundancy and resilience
Policy governance
The first task is to undertake a comprehensive audit of all government digital systems. In addition to identifying dependencies in the cloud, productivity, and analytical layer, it can recommend alternative indigenous tech replacements and upgrades.

The sovereign tech mandate should be the use of Indian-owned platforms for:
Email collaboration (Zoho Mail, Cliq)
Office productivity (Zoho writer, worksheet, show)
CRM and workflow (Freshworks, Kissflow)
Cloud hosting (CtrlS, ESDS)

The government should invent a PLI-like tax break for vendors offering sovereign solutions and incentivize organizations opting for sovereign workspace solutions. CDAC and other Indian IT companies should be encouraged to:
Build parallel systems with Indian cloud providers
Maintain offline backups and hybrid infrastructure




5. Comparative Analysis

The table below gives a comparative analysis of data sovereignty and the impacts of EU and US sanctions on using products from foreign versus Indian IT products.

| Platform | Jurisdiction | Sovereign Control | Sanctions Risk | Suitability for Govt | 
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | 
| Microsoft Azure | U.S. | Low | High | Risky | 
| Zoho | India | High | Low | Ideal | 
| CtrlS | India | High | Low | Ideal | 
| Infosys (on Azure) | India/U.S. | Medium | Medium | Vulnerable | 

It underlines the urgent requirements of data sovereignty in digital products.  

* **National Security**: Reduced exposure to foreign coercion
* **Economic Growth**: Boost to Indian tech ecosystem
* **Public Trust**: Citizens assured of data privacy and continuity
* **Global Leadership**: India sets precedent for digital Sovereignty


 7. Conclusion

India must treat its digital infrastructure as a strategic asset. Sovereign platforms are not just a technical choice but a geopolitical necessity. The time to act is now.
