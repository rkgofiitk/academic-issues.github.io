## India's Digital Sovereignty

[Blog Index](../index.md)

 Abstract

India's digital infrastructure is vulnerable to the blocking of cloud service and workspace APIs by big tech companies like  Microsoft, Google, Meta, Oracle, SAP, and IBM. Recently, the Indian refinery at Nayara in Gujarat suddenly experienced a digital outage. Citing EU sanctions, Microsoft suspended cloud services to Nayara without prior notice for importing Russian crude. The incident is a wake-up call for India to fast-track the implementation of cloud services on indigenous home-grown platforms like Zoho, CtrlS, Meghdoot,  JioCloud, NeuCloud, and encourage the development of a cloud workspace suite based on open source alternatives.


 <strong>1. Background and Strategic Context</strong>

Russia invaded Ukraine on February 24, 2022. The Russian invasion was provoked by Ukraine's political ambitions for integration with NATO and the European Union. President Zelensky wanted to distance Ukraine from Russian influence. However, Russia viewed Ukrainian ambitions as a ploy by NATO to encircle it. Based on the 2001 census and regional data, approximately 30-35% of Ukraine's territory in the east and south is predominantly inhabited by the Russian-speaking people. Geographical regions Kyiv, Lviv, and Kharkiv control Ukraine's political and administrative power. People in the Russian speaking areas were unhappy with an oppressive Ukrainian state regime. The distribution of Russian-speaking areas is as follows (source: [wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Ukraine)).

Overall, about 29.6% of Ukrainians identified Russian as their native language. 

On July 18, 2025, the European Union announced severe sanctions against countries dealing with Russia. Citing the EU sanctions,  on July 24, 2025, Microsoft unilaterally suspended cloud services to Nayara Energy without prior notice. Nayara is an Indian refinery with Russian oil major Rosneft having a 49% stake. Due to Microsoft's sudden actions, the oil tankers waiting at the ports could not get clearance to sail. The incident exposed the fragility of foreign tech dependencies.

The Government of India (GoI) should consider the Nayara incident of Microsoft's digital blocking as a wake-up call. Many critical GoI operations depend on cloud and workspace suites offered by the US-based tech companies. These include revenue collection and many essential operations of government, such as:

- CBDT (Income Tax Business Application)
- GSTN (Goods and Services Tax Network)
- MCA21 (Corporate Affairs Portal)
- UIDAI (Aadhaar infrastructure)

The primary responsibility of  the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) are the collection of: 
- Personal income tax
- Corporate tax
- Securities transaction tax
  
CBDT net annual collection is approximately INR 222600 billion, which is 7.2% of India's GDP. 

The Goods and Services Tax Network (GSTN) is the authority responsible for collecting GST. Its net annual collection is approximately INR 203600 billion, 6.6% of GDP

MCA21 is the main corporate compliance and administration portal. It allows stakeholders -- companies, professionals, and citizens -- to access 
- corporate data,
- statutory documents, and
- file compliance documents from anywhere.
  
It enables MCA to monitor fraud, track compliance, and bring total transparency in the administration of corporate affairs.

UIDAI (Unified Identification Authority of India) is the statutory authority responsible for implementing and maintaining individual Aadhar biometric-based digital identification data. It recorded nearly 2.1 billion transactions for biometric verifications in May 2025. The Aadhaar verification system manages citizens' access to government benefits, subsidies, and biometric authentication-based fintech services. 


Although the critical system modules were implemented by Indian IT companies such as TCS, Infosys, HCL, and Wipro, they use various licensed products, such as Microsoft Azure Platform, Oracle, IBM enterprise products, and Google Workspace. Therefore, many critical functionalities may stop working or experience delays if dependence on foreign tech companies is not eliminated. Though it is unlikely that big tech companies will take the disastrous route of unilateral blocking, the Nayara incident reveals a possibility.  
 

<strong>2. Risk Assessment</strong>

To understand the real impacts, let us analyze the risks of using foreign tech products, especially in digital services. The use of digital systems transcends from education to the economy to modern warfare. Sindoor operation highlighted the capabilities of AI-activated India's air-defence and offensive systems of the IAF. It also underlined Indian IT's expertise in building integrated AI-assisted systems to meet the challenges of modern warfare. 

Indian fintech companies and startups have created an efficient, scam-free finance and investment ecosystem within the reach of citizens. Remote education platforms like Swayam, eVidya, Diksha platforms, and NPTL MOOC are helping to provide high-end learning to poor students in remote villages in India. However, the government policy makers should be aware of dependencies on foreign products in Indian's digital space. They should plan to utilize the technical capabilities and expertise of Indian IT companies. A summary of the risk assessment is given in the table below. 

| Risk Type | Description | Impact Level | 
| --- | --- | --- | 
| License Breach | Foreign vendors can suspend services unilaterally | High | 
| Data Sovereignty | Sensitive citizen data stored in foreign clouds | High | 
| Compliance Overreach | Subject to U.S./EU sanctions and export controls | High | 
| Operational Paralysis | Disruption of tax filings, refunds, compliance | Critical | 


<strong>3. Strategic Objectives</strong>

India should work towards eliminating the influence of big tech companies in its digital ecosystem. Nayara-like incident is neither the first nor will be the last instance of denial of digital access to India.
Both Google and Meta removed Indian government or political content from YouTube and Facebook, citing global practices without legal mandates. Indian content creators were often blocked out, citing compliance with US and EU norms or instances of foreign jurisdiction. 

Oracle licensing terms allow the US courts to exercise jurisdiction over Indian users. Many instances of Microsoft denying Azure Data localization on the pretext of technical infeasibility. Indian defence and public sector establishments have experienced long delays in accessing specialized features or using MS Office macros. Therefore, India should plan for the following strategic goals:
- Ensure uninterrupted access to critical government platforms
- Preserve data sovereignty under Indian jurisdiction
- Reduce/eliminate exposure to foreign sanctions and compliance regimes
- Promote indigenous tech innovation and employment


<strong>4. Proposed Framework</strong>

India should evolve a framework of Indian Data Sovereignty around the following five pillars:
- Audit and Assessments
- Sovereign Tech Mandate 
- Incentives for Migration 
- Redundancy and resilience
- Policy governance
  
The first task is to undertake a comprehensive audit of all government digital systems. In addition to identifying dependencies in the cloud, productivity, and analytical layer, it can recommend alternative indigenous tech replacements and upgrades.

The sovereign tech mandate should be the use of Indian-owned platforms for:
- Email collaboration (Zoho Mail, Cliq)
- Office productivity (Zoho writer, worksheet, show)
- CRM and workflow (Freshworks, Kissflow)
- Cloud hosting (CtrlS, ESDS)

The government should invent a PLI-like tax break for vendors offering sovereign solutions and incentivize organizations opting for sovereign workspace solutions. CDAC and other Indian IT companies should be encouraged to:
Build parallel systems with Indian cloud providers
Maintain offline backups and hybrid infrastructure

<strong>5. Comparative Analysis</strong>

The table below gives a comparative analysis of data sovereignty and the impacts of EU and US sanctions on using products from foreign versus Indian IT products.

| Platform | Jurisdiction | Sovereign Control | Sanctions Risk | Suitability for Govt | 
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | 
| Microsoft Azure | U.S. | Low | High | Risky | 
| Zoho | India | High | Low | Ideal | 
| CtrlS | India | High | Low | Ideal | 
| Infosys (on Azure) | India/U.S. | Medium | Medium | Vulnerable | 

It underlines the urgent requirements of data sovereignty in digital products.  
- **National Security**: Reduced exposure to foreign coercion
- **Economic Growth**: Boost to Indian tech ecosystem
- **Public Trust**: Citizens assured of data privacy and continuity
- **Global Leadership**: India sets precedent for digital Sovereignty


 <strong>7. Conclusion</strong>

Digital infrastructure are a strategic asset for any sovereign country. It should fall prey to foreign dominance and compromize citizens access to privacy or ability of a foreign entity to blackmail the nation. Therefore, sovereign platforms are not just a technical choice but a geopolitical necessity.
