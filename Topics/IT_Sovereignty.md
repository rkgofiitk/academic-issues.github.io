## Watershed moments in India's Tech Sovereignty. 

[Blog Index](../index.md)

In the sequel to the earlier post on [Data Sovereignty](./Tech_Sovereignty.md),  in this article, we analyze the expected disruption in India's IT landscape if the US implements the announced fee hike in H1B visa applications from 2026. Several big tech CEOs including Microsoft, Google, and Tesla & SpaceX came to the US under an H1B visa. Indira Nooyi, the former CEO of Pepsico, also landed in the US with an H1B visa. Perplexity CEO Srinivas Narayanan and AppDynamics (subsequently bought by Cisco) CEO Jyoti Bansal also entered the US using H1B visas. About 73% of H1B visa holders are Indian, 16% are Chinese, and only 3% are Koreans and Canadians. So, the surprise announcement of a hike in visa fees means the opportunities and immigration routes to US is over for the Indians and Chinese.  

The problem with China is different from that of India. China has become the world's manufacturing engine since the early 1990s after Deng Xiao Peng opened China to foreign investments. Still, many of the H1B visa holders from China eventually take the route to settle in the US primarily due to concerns with the CCP. Although there are no authentic statistics of Indians and Chinese seeking the H1B visa route to US citizenship, a rough estimate is 47% and 56% respectively. Annually, about 50k Indian H1B visa holders join the queue to seek immigration. The US issues only 9.8k employment-based green cards due to 7% cap per country. The backlog in employment-based green cards for Indians is 1.2 million as of 2024.

Having set the background, let us analyze how it affects India's [Tech Sovereignty](./Tech_Sovereignty.md). The architects of the spokes and wheels of all big tech companies are people of Indian origin. So, what prevented India from creating 

- A mobile phone brand like Samsung, Apple, or Motorola
- An operating system such as Unix or Microsoft Windows.
- A cloud service ecosystem like Google.

There are many reasons behind the issue. Let me mention a few of these hindrances:

- India did not create the infrastructure base for building big tech companies.
- India did not have an ecosystem of investors and venture capitalists.
- Its license raj and corrupt system of governance did not encourage investors beyond Tatas, Birlas, Mahindras, Munjals, Bajajs, and Hindujas.
- Reliance came up as a textile house in 1966. Adani began diversifying only after mid 1990s.  
- In 1991, some state governments started implementing the software park projects in an uncoordinated, unplanned manner.
- Only undivided Andhra Pradesh had created a comprehensive implementation policy for big investments.
- Even now many states have no transparent framework for investment or startups

Bangalore developed during [SM Krishna's time](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/india/sm-krishna-former-cm-who-transformed-bengaluru-from-pensioners-paradise-to-a-global-it-hub-12886398.html), when the government seriously started the implementation of STPIs in 1991. Despite ill governance and corruption, many things contributed to Bangalore being the high-tech capital of India. The Government of India invested early in many public sector firms,  such as  BEL, HAL, ISRO, HMT, and ITI. Bangalore's Electronic City came up in 1973. The other factors contributing to Bangalore's ascendency as the Silicon Valley of India are the comfortable climate and the excellent research ecosystem with IISc, NAL, ISRO satellite center, DRDO's CAIR, and the Indian Academy of Sciences. Besides Bangalore, Hyderabad is the only city that has consciously developed into a destination for IT firms. [Chandrababu Naidu aggressively wooed investors](https://gunturtdp.com/how-chandrababu-built-hitec-city/) through liberalized policies offering world-class infrastructure in his first stint as Andhra CM. Gujarat developed as a [manufacturing](https://www.shoutnhike.com/blog/gujarat-indias-manufacturing-powerhouse/) and [investment](https://www.investindia.gov.in/state/gujarat) destination during CM Narendra Modi's tenure. Now, Bangalore has outgrown its capacity. Travel from one point to another takes at least 2 hours. Recently, Blackbuck moved out of Bangalore due to its road conditions. The current CM has requested that Ajeemji Premji allow traffic movement inside the Wipro campus. Such ad hoc, capricious thinking of the leaders may lead Bangalore to death.  

[GIFT City](https://giftgujarat.in/) is the recent foray in infrastructure building in Gujarat that started in 2008 under the leadership of then CM Narendra Modi. It has slowly developed as an International Financial Service Center Hub, with cross-border payments, forex trading, and offshore wealth management. It offers 100% tax-free operation, no GST, and a world-class support infrastructure. India's [fintech companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_technology_in_India) have created a unique system of a cleaner ecosystem of financial governance and a micro-payment system. Although the measures are supposed to reduce the role of the [parallel economy](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1513203), currency circulation has increased because of an expanding economy, and many high-value transactions are still in currency. Most of the transactions are related to real estate transactions. 

Absence of work culture among blue colored staff is plaguing the industrial climate.  Frequent work stoppages and vandalism by disgruntled workers often prompt investors to back out.   The freebies culture creates a sense of complacency among the masses. Social security is needed, but not when a person is in their productive age.  There is no place for free lunches. Allowing free bus rides, giving free phones, TVs,  and a monthly stipend for not doing anything is a crime against the taxpayers. 

The Indian government started the Make in India push only in 2014. In the last 3 years, the government has started realizing the importance of and methodology for building tech infrastructure. 
- The startup revolution was initiated under the PLI scheme in 2016,
- In 2021, the government announced that the DLI scheme was for semiconductor manufacturing.
- In 2023,  India launched the  AI mission.
- In 2016, the UPI micropayment system revolutionized currency-less transactions to check widespread scams and fraud. 

The lengthy background is for people to appreciate the role of academic institutions, such as IITs, IISc, AIMS, and IIMs. These institutes produced brilliant graduates with innovative ideas and research motivations. In contrast, the industry lagged behind with problems ranging from an old, archaic system of governance to the tyranny of the license raj and the associated corruption. Every government agency, from non-cooperative bureaucracy to illiterate, corrupt politicians and a laggard Judicial system, conspired to keep people shackled in poverty and lack of education forever. 

What do we need to do? H1B visa fee hike is a watershed moment for the reforms that will accelerate the Indian Big Tech canvas.  
-  Build GIFT city-like infrastructure across the country.
- Assure a consistent and low government intervention to investors and fund managers.
-  But ensure an iron hand when dealing with BluSmart and Gensol engineering scams.
- Allow lateral entry of tech experts to the highest bureaucracy. 
- For example, co-opt Mohandas Pai, Nandal Nilekani, and Shridhar Vembu in policy implementation.
- Invest and create investment opportunities for the cloud ecosystem, like Google.
- Incentivize Zoho, Jio, Tata, CDAC, Bharati Airtel, Adani, and L&T to bring investments.
- Lift the salary cap on Big Tech employees. 

We should understand that ISRO's model of innovations will not succeed across the board. ISRO happened due to Vikram Sarabhai, Satish Dhawan, and US sanctions against scientists. Fewer opportunities elsewhere forced them to build institutions in India.   Similarly, DRDO became successful in missile tech due to Abdul Kalam Azad. Mavericks like Bhaba, Sarbhai, Dhawan, or Azad are found rarely in government labs or find a chance to head the system. We need far-sighted leaders like Ashwani Vaishnavi, who was the principal behind improving railways and train infrastructure despite limitations. Someone in government has to spot an Oppenheimer to lead the challenge in this watershed moment. 

Although the Indian IT outsourcing story became a talking point, we should appreciate that the success was energized by Infosys, Satyam, TCS, Wipro, and HCL after George Fernadez shut out IBM. Let us not have a false pretension that a few individuals' efforts will create a new software ecosystem out of a vacuum. We need an accelerated framework with sufficient government-backed enablers. The right point is to start with academic infrastructure and investment enablers.
- PLI scheme is good,  but ensure investors chip in
- Only 1 out of 10 startups, only 1 becomes a hit. 
- There are not many high-level academic institutions like IISc.
- Upgrade old IITs to research innovation centers.
- Remove Medical Sciences and Law schools from IITs.
- Allow IITs, CFTIs to disburse research initiation grants and matching grants to faculty.
- Create NSF-like liberal but responsible high end research funding.
- Encourage innovations  in the private sector
- Reconstitute HECI as a constitutionally mandated body.
