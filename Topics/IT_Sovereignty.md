## Watershed moments in India's Tech Sovereignty. 

[Blog Index](../index.md)

In the sequel to my earlier post on [Data Sovereignty](./Tech_Sovereignty.md),  in this article, let us analyze the expected disruption in India's IT landscape if the US implements the proposed fee hike in H1B visa applications from 2026. Several big tech CEOs, including Microsoft, Google, and Tesla & SpaceX, came to the US under an H1B visa. About 73% of H1B visa holders are Indian, 16% are Chinese, and only 3% are Koreans and Canadians. So, the surprise announcement of a hike in visa fees means a freeze on the opportunities and "adjustment of status" route to US immigration for the Indians and Chinese.  

The problem with China is different from that of India. China has become the world's largest manufacturing engine since the early 1990s after Deng Xiaoping opened China to foreign investments. Still, many of the H1B visa holders from China eventually apply for adjustment of status to settle in the US primarily due to concerns with the CCP. Although there are no authentic statistics of Indians and Chinese seeking adjustment of status route to US citizenship, rough estimates are 47% and 56% respectively. Annually, about 50k Indian H1B visa holders join the queue to seek adjustment of status. The US issues only 9.8k employment-based green cards due to 7% cap per country. The backlog in employment-based green cards for Indians is roughly 1.2 million as of 2024.

Having set the background, let us analyze how it affects India's [Tech Sovereignty](./Tech_Sovereignty.md). The key creators of the spokes and wheels of most big tech companies in US are people of Indian origin. So, what has prevented India from developing
- A mobile phone brand like Samsung, Apple, or Motorola
- An operating system such as Unix or Microsoft Windows.
- A cloud service ecosystem like Google or Amazon.

There are many reasons behind the issue. Let me mention a few of these hindrances:
- India did not create the infrastructure base for building big tech companies.
- India did not have an ecosystem of investors and venture capitalists.
- License raj and corrupt governance did not encourage investors beyond Tatas, Birlas, Mahindras, Munjals, Bajajs, and Hindujas.
- Reliance came up as a textile house in 1966. Adani began diversifying only after the mid-1990s.  
- In 1991, some state governments started implementing the software park projects in a very uncoordinated, unplanned manner.
- Only undivided Andhra Pradesh had created a comprehensive implementation policy for large investments.
- Even now, most Indian states have no transparent framework for investment or startups.

Bangalore developed during [SM Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._M._Krishna)'s time, when the government seriously started the implementation of STPIs in 1991. Despite ill governance and corruption, many things contributed to Bangalore becoming the high-tech capital of India. The Government of India invested early in many public sector firms,  such as  BEL, HAL, [ISRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISRO), HMT, and ITI. Bangalore's [Electronic City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_City) came up in 1978. The other factors contributing to Bangalore's ascendency as the Silicon Valley of India are the comfortable climate and the excellent research ecosystem led by IISc, NAL, ISRO satellite center, DRDO's CAIR, and the Indian Academy of Sciences. Besides Bangalore, Hyderabad is the only city that has consciously developed into a destination for IT firms. [Chandrababu Naidu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N._Chandrababu_Naidu) aggressively wooed investors through liberalized policies offering world-class infrastructure in his first stint as Andhra CM. Now, Bangalore has outgrown its capacity. Travel from one point to another takes at least two hours. Recently, Blackbuck's CEO threatened to move out of Bangalore due to the condition of its roads. Karnataka CM requested [Azim Premji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azim_Premji) to allow traffic movement inside the Wipro campus to ease traffic on the outer ring road. Azim Premji has predictably rejected the request. But the point is that the leaders' ad hoc, capricious thinking will lead Bangalore to death.  

[GIFT City](https://giftgujarat.in/) is the only recent notable effort in infrastructure building in Gujarat. It started in 2008 under the leadership of then CM [Narendra Modi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narendra_Modi). It has quickly developed into an International Financial Service Center Hub, offering cross-border payments, forex trading, and offshore wealth management. It provides 100% tax-free operation, no GST, and a world-class support infrastructure. India's fintech companies have created a unique system of clean financial governance and a [micro-payment system](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/m/micropayment.asp). Although the measures are supposed to reduce the role of the [parallel economy](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1513203), the currency circulation has increased. The expert attributes it to an expanding economy, and many high-value transactions, such as those related to real estate,  are still in currency. Dealing with lower bureaucracy in India is a nightmarish experience for honest citizens.

Absence of a decent work culture among blue-collar staff is plaguing the industrial climate. Frequent work stoppages and vandalism by disgruntled workers often prompt investors to scale down or ship out.   The freebies culture creates a sense of complacency among the masses. Social security is needed, but not when a person is in their productive age. There is no place for free lunches. Allowing free bus rides, giving free phones, TVs,  and a monthly stipend (for not doing anything) is a crime against the taxpayers. It is like snatching money from one to bribe another. Unfortunately, neither the ECI nor the courts have anything to say on this systematic snatching and bribing.

The Indian government started the Make in India push only in 2014. In the last 3 years, the government has started realizing the importance of and methodology for building tech infrastructure. 
- The startup revolution was initiated under the [PLI scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_Linked_Incentive_schemes_in_India) in 2016,
- In 2021, the government announced that the [DLI scheme](https://chips-dli.gov.in/) was for semiconductor manufacturing.
- In 2023,  India launched the  [AI mission](https://www.india.gov.in/website-indiaai-mission).
- In 2016, the UPI micropayment system revolutionized currency-less transactions to check widespread scams and fraud. 

The lengthy background is for people to appreciate the role of academic institutions, such as IITs, IISc, AIMS, and IIMs. These institutes produced brilliant graduates with innovative ideas and research motivations. In contrast, the industry lagged behind with problems ranging from an old, archaic system of governance to the tyranny of the license raj and the associated corruption. Every government agency, from non-cooperative bureaucracy to illiterate, corrupt politicians and a laggard Judicial system, conspired to keep people shackled in poverty and lack of education forever. 

What do we need to do? H1B visa fee hike is a watershed moment for the reforms that will accelerate the Indian Big Tech canvas.  
-  Build GIFT city-like infrastructure across the country.
- Assure a consistent and low government intervention for investors and fund managers.
-  But ensure an iron hand when dealing with BluSmart and Gensol engineering scams.
- Allow lateral entry of tech experts to the highest bureaucracy. 
- For example, co-opt [Mohandas Pai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._Mohandas_Pai), [Nandan Nilekani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandan_Nilekani), and [Shridhar Vembu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sridhar_Vembu) in policy implementation.
- Invest and create investment opportunities for the cloud ecosystem, like Google.
- Incentivize Zoho, Jio, Tata, CDAC, Bharati Airtel, Adani, and L&T to bring investments.
- Lift the salary cap on Big Tech employees.
- Reduce lower bureaucracy's influence with automations of the entire government clearance system.
- Set fixed timeframe for all government clearances.

We should understand that ISRO's model of innovations will not succeed across the board. ISRO happened due to [Vikram Sarabhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikram_Sarabhai), [Satish Dhawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satish_Dhawan), and US sanctions against scientists. Fewer opportunities elsewhere forced them to build institutions in India.   Similarly, DRDO became successful in missile tech due to [APJ Abdul Kalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._P._J._Abdul_Kalam). Mavericks like [Bhaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homi_J._Bhabha), Sarbhai, Dhawan, or Kalam are rarely found in government labs or have a chance to head the system. We need more far-sighted and honest leaders. Someone in government has to spot an "Indian Oppenheimer" to lead the challenge in India's "Soft Pokharan Project" in this watershed moment. 

Although the Indian IT outsourcing story became a talking point, we should appreciate that the success was energized by Infosys, Satyam, TCS, Wipro, and HCL after [George Fernandes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Fernandes) shut out IBM. Let us not have a false pretension that efforts of a few individuals will create a new software ecosystem out of a vacuum. We need an accelerated framework with sufficient government-backed enablers. The right point is to start with reforms in investment, infrastructure, and academic enablers.
- PLI scheme is good,  but ensure investors chip in
- Only 1 out of 10 startups, only 1 becomes a hit. 
- There are not many high-level academic institutions like IISc.
- Upgrade old IITs to research innovation centers.
- Remove Medical Sciences and Law schools from IITs.
- Allow IITs and CFTIs to disburse enough research initiation and matching grants to faculty.
- Create NSF-like liberal but responsible high-end research funding.
- Encourage innovations  in the private sector
- [Reconstitute HECI](./IEC_Proposal.md) as a constitutionally mandated body.

I am happy to read the news that Sridhar Vembu has declared that Zoho is ready to scale its SaaS to the level of Google. [Ashwini Vaishnaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashwini_Vaishnaw) has stated that he has migrated to Zoho's service. Let the other powerful people in government take a not of it.
