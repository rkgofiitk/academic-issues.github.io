# Using AI for Assistance

[Blog Index](../index.md)

Over last couple years, the awareness of the power of Generative AI has caught imagination of people even when many of them found using many features of a smart phone technically challenging. Many have developed capabilities to solve a difficult situation arising out of a source where they don't have competence. As one of the leading musician thought Gen AI triggers the creativity. People find dialogue with Gen AI quite engaging and culminates by drawing best out of creative engagement. Investment bancking, regulatory compliance for tax liabilities, and tackling legal issues are some of the important applications where people extensively use assistance of Generative AI.  The other extensive use of Generative AI is in content creation, reports generation and legal contract reviews. HR departments in corporate sectors have started using AI tools for hiring, performance reviews, and layoffs.  People have now started using Gen AI even in creative areas like painting, visual arts, music and audio, video and animations. Shankar Mahadevan, one leading musicians of India thinks that Gen AI helpong musicians to triggers creativity. The success and potentials of Generative AI has laid foundations for exploration of Agentic AI and General AI (GAI) or Artificial General AI (AGI).  Driver-less cars, industrial automation, precision target hittings in battle field, rapid prototyping, robot-assisted surgery, assisted-livings, represent a few notable use of AGI.  Agentic AI is one of the key framework for large-scale deployment of AGI.

Agentic AI systems are proactive rather than reactive and work with minimal or no intervention by their human handlers. Agents are context-aware, learn continuously from feedback and experience. They are able to perceive their environment, reason about the options, and refine their approach dynamically. Agentic AI system can build/create workflows with networks of multiple AI agents that collaborate together to solve complex, real-world problems. Agents can migrate. However, agent migration is tricky, especially, if it crosses network boundaries. It has many things in common with classical distributed agent systems. The hype about Agentic AI is driven by its promise of flexible autonomy and seamless orchestration of resources leveraging LLM and reinforcement learning. However, the bottom-line of operation of an Agentic AI system are the underlying algorithms. An algorithmic system cannot completely replicate human cognition even with LLM. A key challenge for deploying Agentic AI will be to develop a framework of sustainable AI goverance.

Without effective governance AI could exacerbate **economic disparities, cyber threats, and ethical violations**. Let us start with the objectives of AI governance.

- Establish a **binding global AI ethics framework**.
- Automate real-time **AI auditing and risk assessment**.
- Prevent misuse in **high-risk sectors**, such as lethal weapons.
- Strengthen defenses against **AI-driven cyber attacks**.
- Create **global counter-AI intelligence systems**.
- Ensure **fair AI access to all nations**.
- Prevent monopolization of **AI-driven financial systems**.
- Promote **open-source AI innovation**.

It may appear as an unrealizable wish list to many, but we still need to work with dilligence towards it. Otherwise, the gap between haves and have-nots will reach an unsustainable proportion leading to lage-scale disruptions of global order. Most difficult objective is establishing a binding global AI ethics framework. One may say that we still have to develop a sufficiently strong ethical framework for computing and application of computing. Under the circumstance, the talk about ethics of governing AI systems sound premature. Ethics should not be confused with surveillance or policing. It helps us raise red flags and apply course corrections. The course correction is a not simple. It may include pressurizing rough countries and enterprise to adhere to mutual cooperation boundaries for preserving global order. In this context, a clear policy guidelines for fairness, transparency, accountability and protecting human rights are the key elements. In other words, we need a sort of standardization of these guidelines to detect violations. It should be a sort of mechanism for a sustainable global societal system. Digital data protection laws are foundational pillars for the same. European General Data Protection Rules (GDPR) is one of the finest document in this respect. Indian DPDPA is comparable to GDPR. It is consent-centric and has more stricter provision for reporting breaches. However, the major problem with India's regulatory system is enforceability as is with any other digitally less aware nations. Most of the other objectives are centered around the ethical AI governance system. 

The second objective of AI governance is not difficult to implement, if a real-time automated monitoring system is incorportated along with the AI system. For large data center operations, automated health monitoring systems are already in use. Only difference is to embed ethics violations rules to these monitoring system. The third objective is a bit unenforceable. However, we can compare it with Nuclear weapons deterance doctrine that most countries follow. Even after many threats of using controlled nuclear devices in Ukraine war, Russia did not actually use it. We may rely on rational thinking of political leaders for implementation of this objective. Howerver, I see a problem in implementing the fourth objective. Cyber attacks are individual centric activities and less controllable from a central entities. Defense mechanisms peocisw only guardrails against it. So the underlying approach for mitigating the menace is to develop AI enabled anti-virus softwares. The next objective is a more nuanced incarnation of cyber attack mitigation. However, counter intelligence system also require data gathering in hostile environment. It is more often than not a cyber attack without mainfestation of consequential damages in immediate time frame. I feel fair access of AI to all nations is rooted in encouraging open-source AI innovations. Developing AI systems is highly resource-intensive. so I see a problem in development of open source AI systems unless governments of many nations come together. There many organized group of nations like European Union, BRICS, G20, G7, etc. AI Action Summit is a global forum created explicitly for AI governance. The AI action summit 2025 has issued a joint declaration on inclusive and sustainable AI signed by 58 countries. However, UK and US did not endorse it citing excessive AI regulations. So there is always a problem of disagreement among nations who have capabilities to develop AI tools. 

Deep Seek a an open source Gen AI tool. Deep Seek-V3 is hosted on Github allowing developers to access, study and improve its architecture, modify and contribute. One can download and install in local machine. However, it becomes extremely slow when run on a local machine. So, Deep Seek is the first attempt to democratize the use of a Gen AI tool.
