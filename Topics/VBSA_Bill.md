## Vikashit Bharat Shiksha Adhikshan Bill

[Blog Index](../index.md)

What is in a name? Thus spake Juliet in the iconic Shakespearean play Romeo and Juliet. The Government of India thinks otherwise. The Union Cabinet approved a unified bill to replace the UGC, NCTE, and AICTE with a single regulatory body. It will bring [non-medical and non-law education under the ambit of this overarching body](https://www.timesnownews.com/education/centre-approves-bill-to-replace-ugc-aicte-and-ncte-article-153284575). Although there has been noise about HECI from 2018, not much movement was seen until now. It is unclear whether sufficient consultation has been conducted among stakeholders before creating the contours of the bill. The real intention behind the bill is to [aggregate regulatory oversight into an overarching authority](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/viksit-bharat-shiksha-adhikshan-bill-the-catastrophic-implications-for-higher-education-in-india) that exercises central control. However, the bill leaves funding under the administrative control of the Ministry.
Furthermore, law and medical education are not under the jurisdiction of the proposed regulatory body. It leaves a lingering doubt that the GoI is trying to create a trap for the autonomy of universities. I have no idea about the fate of central institutions like the IITs, which Acts of Parliament created. If such central institutions are also brought under the oversight of the proposed regulatory bodies, then surely the bill should be christened as "Viparit Budhi Bharat Sisksha Adhikshan Bill."  

 Many educationists believe that it is a way to bring Foreign universities to set up shop in India. A competition is definitely welcome. But the cost of education should be a significant consideration. The Indian middle class has a median salary of 35k-45k per month. It is just sufficient for food and clothing. So, a parent's dream of sending even one child to a decent university would remain unattainable. The government would have a hands-off approach to college fees as it had in the case of unregulated airfare hikes. With no guarantee of a job, the loan burden for college fees will create an unsustainable existential problem for the middle class. The question is: does the government have a plan to tackle the issue?

Some form of central regulatory control is necessary to oversee the low-grade private universities and declining state universities, which are routinely plagued by rampant political interference from the state governments. However, the regulatory body should be backed by [a constitutional guarantee](https://rkgofiitk.github.io/academic-issues.github.io/Topics/NewHECI.html). I guess [the Radhakrishnan committee ](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/isro-ex-chief-led-panel-to-onboard-heis-in-new-regulatory-setup/articleshow/121998309.cms) was tasked with resolving implementation issues and proposing harmonized protocols for merging regulatory mechanisms of UGC, AICTE, and NCTE into a single Higher Education Commission. The committee must have submitted the report by now, and perhaps the Union Cabinet's approval is based on this report. The surprising fact is that the Radhakrishnan committee's report has not yet been made public. At least, I have not come across the report. In my earlier blog on the Radhakrishnan Committee, I mentioned two critical issues related to the proposal to establish HECI. 
What is the basic purpose of creating a centralized single agency like the Higher Education Council of India?
Why does the government feel that multiple regulatory agencies are inadequate to supervise and control the quality of education?

The VBSA bill must address the two points above and other implementation-related protocols. However, efforts to micromanage institutions of higher learning through governmental fiat will have ruinous effects, rather than raising quality.  Furthermore, it is concerning that medical and legal education have been excluded from the proposed HECI's scope, which undermines our confidence in the government's intentions. The approach reflects a political pragmatism. The government has chosen a less contentious target in proposing the HECI bill. Obviously, the government is underprepared to confront the powerful lobby of the legal and medical professions. 

Assuming that the Radhakrishnan committee's report serves as the basis for the new VBSA bill, the committee has thoroughly examined the micro-details of implementation issues and suggested strong countermeasures against political and bureaucratic interferences. If not, the bill may undermine the well-intentioned approach to skill building and disrupt the country's higher education system.
