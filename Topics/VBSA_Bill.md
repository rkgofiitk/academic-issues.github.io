## Vikashit Bharat Shiksha Adhikshan Bill

[Blog Index](../index.md)

What is in a name? Thus spake Juliet in the iconic Shakespearean play Romeo and Juliet. The Government of India thinks otherwise. The Union Cabinet approved a unified bill to replace the UGC, NCTE, and AICTE with a single regulatory body. It will bring [non-medical and non-law education under the ambit of this overarching body](https://www.timesnownews.com/education/centre-approves-bill-to-replace-ugc-aicte-and-ncte-article-153284575). Although there has been noise about HECI from 2018, not much movement was seen until now. It is unclear whether sufficient consultation has been conducted among stakeholders before creating the contours of the bill. The real intention behind the bill is to [aggregate regulatory oversight into an overarching authority](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/viksit-bharat-shiksha-adhikshan-bill-the-catastrophic-implications-for-higher-education-in-india) that exercises central control. However, the bill leaves funding under the administrative control of the Ministry.
Furthermore, law and medical education are not under the jurisdiction of the proposed regulatory body. It leaves a lingering doubt that the GoI is trying to create a trap for the autonomy of universities. I have no idea about the fate of statutory central institutions like IITs, created by an Act of Parliament. If such central institutions are also brought under the proposed regulatory bodies, then surely the bill should be christened as "Viparit Bharat Sisksha Adhikshan Bill."  

Some form of central regulatory control is necessary to oversee the low-grade private universities and declining state universities, which are often plagued by rampant political interference from state governments. However, the regulatory body should be backed by [a constitutional guarantee](https://rkgofiitk.github.io/academic-issues.github.io/Topics/NewHECI.html). I guess [the Radhakrishnan committee ](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/isro-ex-chief-led-panel-to-onboard-heis-in-new-regulatory-setup/articleshow/121998309.cms) was tasked with resolving implementation issues and proposing harmonized protocols for merging regulatory mechanisms of UGC, AICTE, and NCTE into a single Higher Education Commission. The committee must have submitted the report by now, and perhaps the Union Cabinet's approval is based on this report. The surprising fact is that the Radhakrishnan committee's report has not yet been made public. At least, I have not come across the report. In my earlier blog on the Radhakrishnan Committee, I mentioned two critical issues related to the proposal to establish HECI. 
What is the basic purpose of creating a centralized single agency like the Higher Education Council of India?
Why does the government feel that multiple regulatory agencies are inadequate to supervise and control the quality of education?
The VBSA bill must have addressed these two and other implementation-related protocols. However, the efforts to micromanage institutions of higher learning through Governmental fiat will only have a ruinous effect instead ot raising the quality. It is also noteworthy that medical and legal education have been excluded from the scope of the proposed HECI. It does not boost our confidence in the government's intentions. However, political pragmatism is on display, as the government chose a soft target in proposing the HECI bill.
