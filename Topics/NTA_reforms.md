## Opaque Audits and Ad Hoc Purges Undermine NTA Exam Credibility 

A massive cleanup took place in NTA following the violent protest at Jantar Mantar on July 20, 2026, engineered by the self-proclaimed GenZ representatives CJP over the NEET2026 paper leak. [It removed 47 officials](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/india/nta-removes-47-officials-as-centre-steps-up-overhaul-amid-paper-leak-row/articleshow/132608498.cms?from=mdr) from service. However, NTA's ability to conduct the exam smoothly under its supervision remained shrouded in controversy amid [fresh reports of bungling in the NET exam](https://education.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/higher-education/ugc-net-2026-nta-to-re-conduct-english-commerce-sociology-papers-over-paper-answer-key-issues/133277848). It has ordered fresh retests on three subjects: English, Commerce, and Sociology. Around 60% of the questions in these papers were repeated from the 2024 papers, and there were many typos, garbled spellings, grammatical errors, punctuation errors, non-standard terminology, and factual errors. 

In my humble opinion (IMHO), a cosmetic purge of officials is only an ad hoc solution to the issue of coordinated paper leaks facilitated by the coaching mafia and a questionable choice of subject experts. In fact, the CBI named the [subject experts as the kingpins of NEET paper leaks](https://lawbeat.in/articles/liberty-in-the-courts-how-the-neet-ug-fast-track-court-order-shields-indias-youth-from-institutional-fraud-1622545). NTA has removed 600 subject experts. Though we don't know the internal details of the removal, one needs to recognize a fundamental distinction between science and humanities exams to understand why reform must be nuanced. The questions in science topics are specification-driven, with limited room for ambiguity. The errors, while damaging, are usually technical.
In contrast, questions in the humanities are open to interpretation. The answers may depend on orientation, ideology, or even an examiner's fancy. I recall serving on a Ph.D. selection panel in which candidates from a particular university were trained to respond only within a narrow orientation. History and philosophy, disciplines meant to broaden minds, had been reduced to rehearsed narratives. This is why humanities exams are especially vulnerable to compromised experts and biased question-setting.

IMHO, the problem with the smooth conduct of the exam will persist unless NTA fully owns and is accountable for exam integrity, rather than diffusing responsibility across universities, committees, and outsourced vendors. NTA has now proposed a 4-tier paper scrutiny system. The salient points are:
- Complete revamp of CONOPS (confidential operations)
- Strict compliance with established examination protocols
- Onboarding professionals for new leadership positions in the agency
  
One key point missing is the revamping of the audit system. The [professionals the government has decided to onboard](https://education.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/higher-education/govt-orders-overhaul-of-nta-exam-system-directs-audit-of-entire-testing-process/133292812) include a General Manager for Research and Development and Psychometrics, and a Chief Information Security Officer. The move demonstrates the government's seriousness about an in-depth, rigorous post-exam audit. Otherwise, creating such senior positions in the agency's hierarchy does not make sense. 

The current post-exam audit system is in 4 layers:
- 30-Day Corrective Action Audit
- Independent Review Panel Intervention
- Multi-Stage Biometric Cross-Checking
- Psychometrics and Technology Evaluation

The first three layers are primarily intended to ensure the sanctity and integrity of exam conduct. The inputs collected by these layers improve the integrity of the exam system and its protocols. It is the last layer that provides meaningful input to make the exam paper student-friendly. 

I feel that the opaque, centralized audit system is the Achilles heel of the current system. IMHO, the government is silent on three important issues.
- Not sharing the audit reports publicly.
- Over-reliance on outsourcing security and the conduct of examinations.
- Reactive rather than proactive error and validation mechanisms

Keeping audit findings locked inside the agency erodes credibility. Publishing anonymized audit reports would restore public confidence and hold specific officials within the agency accountable. The reliance on outsourcing is not merely a symptom of weak capacity—it has become a convenient way to evade accountability. Despite being established in 2017, NTA still lacks in‑house expertise, forcing it to delegate core functions to external vendors. This outsourcing produces a reactive approach to error detection and underscores the ownership problem: NTA cannot credibly claim accountability while outsourcing the very processes it is meant to safeguard.

In a related development, NTA's reforms on NEET-UG came under the Supreme Court's scrutiny. During a hearing on [the plea of FIAMA and UDF seeking structural reforms in NTA](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/education/news/supreme-court-puts-nta-on-the-spot-over-neet-reforms-no-more-ad-hoc-measures-as-exam-system-faces-major-changes/articleshow/133343562.cms), the court sought the government's response on the implementation of the K. Radhakrishnan committee's recommendations. It expressed dissatisfaction with the ad hoc measures while ignoring the implementation of the committee's recommendations. The Supreme Court’s intervention highlights a larger truth: reform committees lose their value if the recommendations are not actively implemented. Implementation, not proliferation, is the real test of credibility. Unless NTA moves beyond ad hoc purges and opaque audits, its reforms will remain ritualistic, and India’s students will continue to pay the price for institutional failure.
