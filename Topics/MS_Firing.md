## Microsoft Laysoff 9k to Fuel its Intelligent Engine


Satya Nadella sent a memo to employees about job cuts, laying off 9000 employees in July 2025. Amid Microsoft's record-breaking profit of 75 million USD, Nadella abuses a philosophical term, "the enigma of success," to defend the announcement of job cuts. Microsoft has 228k employees. So the "enigma of success" layoffs constitute 3.95% of its workforce. The term "enigma of success" is used to imply that, despite success, the company has to restructure for accelerated growth. The company must pour 80 billion USD into data centers, chips, and AI talent. The way they could do it is to cut the low end of the food chain, announcing 9000 layoffs of inconsequential existing talent. The employees now wonder if the company's claim of an "empathetic culture" has eroded completely, 

Let us analyze the restructuring issue, which Nadella exuberantly terms an "enigma of success."

- Tech evolution: Investing in the "intelligence engine; means faster innovations, heralding new possibilities and unforeseen frontiers of research
- Accelerate growth: AI will bring comforts, leaving human drudgery behind as a thing of the past.

Tech evolution turns everything into a touchscreen simplicity that enables us to accomplish things faster with smart models with massive scalability. Human involvement reduces to sensory perceptions, such as touching, hearing, seeing, and thinking. It may entirely or largely eliminate the requirement for a workforce. Accelerating growth is an obvious outcome of tech innovation that factors out human drudgery to fuel growth. 
But the question is: Is the cost of fuelling the tech evolution greater than human devolution? 

When a cost is involved, we always look for an optimal solution. Microsoft applied an optimal solution, laying off 9000 employees to fuel its intelligent engine. The average salary of a Microsoft employee is 131k USD, so it can save about 1.81b USD, just about 2.2% of the cost of fueling the intelligent engine. However, saving is not the issue; it is the easiest option for the company.   Some may argue that complicated legal issues will arise out of mass firing. Let us figure out the cost of the legal problems. Is Microsoft prepared to handle it, or could the class action suit by employees end up as the endgame for Nadella's enigma of success?
A top law firm, Sanford Heisler Sharp McKnight, is investigating unlawful layoffs, discriminatory terminations, and violations of the WARN (Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification) Act for a possible class action suit. However, the success of legal petitions against strategic restructuring is complex. The defense that layoffs are for funding AI will not cut much ice in the legal arena. The legal defence team must successfully argue the three key issues:
Unlawful conduct like discrimination, prejudice, or retaliation
Violation of labor laws, such as giving notices under the WARN Act
Breach of contracts and good faith, such as when employees were promised stability but were suddenly terminated.
Sanford Heisler Sharp McKnight knows the legal issues and will investigate them before filing any legal plaint. However, a court battle will drag on, allowing Microsoft enough leg space to claim a restructuring pivot as an enigma of success.

On a different note, let us view the brutal fights we will witness in  AI supremacy. The space of innovation seems to be cramped up with too many players:
- Meta Superintelligence Lab
- Anthropic's Constitutional AI
- Google DeepMind
- OpenAI's Superintelligent team
  
I am not aware of IBM's efforts in the AI space. However, traditionally, IBM has focused on enterprise hardware and software. IBM's AI efforts will be embedded in the enterprise space. Besides the US-based AI innovations, China, India, and the European Union have AI projects.  

In the context of eventualities, the AI race appears like a soft Manhattan Project. AI companies are racing to build a soft cartel to control the governance and finances of nations. The US funded the Manhattan Project with Oppenheimer to garner the strength of nuclear hegemony to annihilate a nation. The subsequent race for acquiring atomic bombs has not been one of the pleasant experiences for humankind. I may sound philosophical, but the case AI race will quickly become a "Soft Manhattan Project!" 

Let us take a moment to assess the appropriateness of the analogy between a soft cartel and a soft Manhattan Project. The straightforward interpretation of "the enigma of successful layoffs" implies that Microsoft is funding its intelligence engine initiative through the savings generated by the salaries of laid-off employees. These layoffs are projected to yield savings of approximately $10 billion over five years, accounting for about 12.5% of the anticipated cost of its intelligent engine. This strategy reflects a typical cartel mentality: reallocating soft disposable spending to secure resources for controlling finances and influencing governance. AI dominance is poised to elevate these companies into a more profound power control structure. I believe that the short-term disruptions in governance caused by AI could be more catastrophic than a thousand wars. I refer to it as a short-term disruption because ultimately, human-generated innovative ideas are likely to prevail over the AI-driven control approach in the long run. However, the intervening period is bound to be painful for a large segment of the population.
