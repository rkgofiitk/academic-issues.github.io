## AI-Enabled Disruptive Interventions/Inventions

[Blog Index](../index.md)

Citrini Research [published a report on 23rd February, 2026](https://www.citriniresearch.com/p/2028gic) that talks about the impact of AI-led disruption on the economy, expanding on two WTF scenarios: 
- What if AI is always right, and
- What if AI is always bearish?

Citrini Research is a small independent financial research firm founded by James van Geelen in 2023. It focuses on in-depth thematic predictions across topics such as AI, Robotics, and GLP-1 drugs to raise awareness of long-term, big-ticket investment trends. In the absence of a proper terminology, we can say that the firm creates "fin-fictions with realistic-looking predictions." It published a 7000-word report on a doomsday prediction involving AI, which forecast a 10% unemployment rate and a 38% drop in the S&P 500 by 2028. It led to the [crash of Indian IT stocks by INR 84000 crores](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/stocks/story/contract-cancellations-at-tcs-infosys-wipro-ghost-gdp-and-sp-500-at-8k-citrini-scenarios-517686-2026-02-24) (approximately 8.5 billion USD) on 24th February. Whether or not Citrini did a 'Hindenburg on Indian IT sector', only time will unravel!! But the comparison is not far‑fetched. We have already witnessed how Hindenburg Research operated. It published a series of negative reports on Adani, engaged in short‑selling, and eventually, Nate Anderson had to shut down Hindenburg's research operations in January 2025 to avoid prosecution. That episode itself is evidence of how financial narratives can be weaponized. Such negative reports trigger stock crashes, leading to the evaporation of wealth, and the authors profit from the turmoil. However, an interesting part of the report is as follows:
- It introduces the concept of a "Ghost GDP."
- Weakening demands to quicken AI deployments.
- Leading to a cascading rise in unemployment rates.

The concept of Ghost GDP is not new. In reality, GDP has always been a "Ghost" and will always remain so, irrespective of who is in government or in opposition. As always, we continue to learn from the opposition that GDP data published by Government agencies are overestimates and manipulations. They won't, and never will, provide any proof to support their claims. Admittedly, the accumulation of wealth by a few large investors or banks is not a reliable indicator of GDP. However, we must understand that a lack of capital terminates the closed loop of wealth generation and investments. Similarly, the cycle of reduced demand and consequential rise in unemployment is also not difficult to understand. Any progress or achievement in AI will require trillions of dollars of investment. It means that unless a nation can generate trillion dollars of spareable wealth, it cannot make any discernible achievements in AI. Therefore, the Citrini Research Doomsday AI prophecy, leading to a drop in the S&P 500 index and a 10% rise in the unemployment rate, is a wee bit pessimistic and may be intended to spur uprisings and instability in South Asian countries, including India. 

[Aswath Damodaran](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/), a highly acclaimed economics professor at [Stern School of Business of New York University](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/), put Citrini's Doomsday AI disruption prediction to his own 3P (possible, plausible, and probable) test. His worst-case analysis of AI disruption [concludes that it may still lead to significant long-term benefits](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/03/ai-scenarios-from-economic-doomsday-to.html). That pours cold water on Citrini's report. The brief takeaways from Prof Damodaran's musings are: 
- The Citrini's report lays out a dark endgame for the economy.
- AI disruption prophecy came out of the pains of a trillion dollars of investment without proportionate gains
- AI disruption has led to job losses for largely white collared, high earners.
- AI adoption is happening across firms quickly, leading to unemployment of high earners.

The Citrini's Doomsday predictor links AI disruption, leading to job losses among high earners, to a large cutback in consumption and the slowing of the economy. However, this may be true in the US or in countries with high GDP figures. In South Asia, except perhaps China, AI adoption will be slow. Of course, the Indian Government has become an enabler for AI adoption in many sectors, including construction, which employs a large number of unorganized workers. In the recently concluded AI Impact Summit, we saw all AI company CEOs, Anthropic, Meta, OpenAI, and Google personally attended and forged several tie-ups with India AI firms for Indian language AI tools. I think AI companies envisioning an anticipated Indian success story could enhance the credibility of AI Doomsday advocates and help them plow back the trillion dollars they have invested.  

Prof Damodaran unveils the journey of AI disruption from "impossibility" to "almost certain." AI advocates keep saying that disruption will happen, but they are not sure when. Until now, there is little evidence of the disruption happening. Citrini's report led to a wipeout of $ 8.5 billion on 24th February, 2026. In fact, many known commentators like [Raghuram Rajan echoed the AI disruption storyline](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/company/corporate-trends/ai-doomsday-scenario-ex-rbi-guv-raghuram-rajan-says-indias-services-sector-will-be-disrupted-not-derailed/articleshow/128841471.cms?from=mdr), impacting the Indian Service sector. However, many of them do make reasoned advocacy but lack sufficient evidence to back up their claims with products and to estimate the probability of their financial success. Prof Damodaran's original article makes a compelling case for the plausibility of AI disruption. Citrini's "fin‑fiction" may succeed in rattling investors, but until it passes the 3P filter, it remains more a provocation than a prediction. The real risk is not AI disruption itself, but the heightened risk of weaponizing financial fiction—stories designed to crash markets first and prove themselves later.
