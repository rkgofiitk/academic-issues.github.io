## Anthropic Shutdown & AI Sovereignty 
### India’s dependence on foreign AI tools exposes the risks of access without ownership.

[Blog Index](../index.md)

On June 12, 2026, the United States government issued directives to Anthropic, mandating the restriction of access to its Fable 5 and Mythos 5 products for all foreign subscribers. This action prompted significant social media commentary about Infosys Chairman Nandan Nilekani's [2-year-old remarks at the "Build with AI Summit"](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/trends/story/why-nandan-nilekani-is-facing-social-media-heat-after-us-anthropic-curbs-536723-2026-06-13) in Bengaluru. Nilekani advocated focusing on practical use cases for Indian IT companies rather than developing additional large language models (LLMs). There are two specific reasons for Nilekani's comment that Indian IT companies do not want to venture into greenfield projects like building a Sovereign LLM. 
- All major Indian IT companies, such as TCS, Wipro, Infosys, HCL, Tech Mahindra, and L&T Infotech, are Public Limited companies and are expected to deliver stable profits to shareholders.
- The combined market capitalization of the top five IT companies of India is about 240 billion USD. The total revenue from the IT industry is roughly 300 billion USD.
     
The discourse on social media suggested that government strategies may be more effective than the recommendations of private-sector IT leaders. However, we should keep in mind that the Indian government's AI investment budget over five years is INR 10,372 crore (approx. 1.1 billion USD), spread across chipsets, datasets, skilling, and subsidies, which is roughly equivalent to the funding needed to build an LLM. In fact, when we compare investments in building LLMs from scratch, we find that India's USD 1.1 billion for five years is less than 2% of the funding raised by companies like OpenAI, Anthropic, and China's DeepSeek. The annual budget outlay for CAPEX projects in India has reached [INR 12.2 lac crore (approx. 134 billion USD)](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2241612&reg=3&lang=1) in 2025-2026. A large chunk of this outlay is driven by infrastructure upgradation requirements, such as roads, railways, shipping, energy, green hydrogen, and also includes semiconductors, electronic manufacturing, and data centers.  So, we should view Nilekani's comments in the proper context rather than trolling him. 

The US government, citing national security concerns, directed Anthropic to [block any non-US subscribers or countries from accessing Fable 5 and Mythos 5](https://www.anthropic.com/news/fable-mythos-access). Anthropic disagrees with the ban, stating that it had no choice but to abruptly shut down Fable 5 and Mythos 5 for all customers. The directives did not provide specific details regarding the security concerns.

One of the main concerns is the potential for these tools to be exploited in coordinated cyberattacks against sensitive security information infrastructure. In fact, Claude and other AI-assisted tools generated 1.5 million concurrent connections to the CBSE OSM portal in just two minutes. Such attacks are usually smoke-screened as denial-of-service attacks, but are actually attempts to access protected files. The Fable 5 and Mythos 5 tools could facilitate high-volume unauthorized access to sensitive information, especially since much of modern warfare is  conducted through command-and-control centers over the Internet from war rooms.

Anthropic has previously worked with the US Military Intelligence and other agencies. The dispute escalated in January 2026, when the company was banned from government contracts. The latest directive, citing a suspected Fable 5 jailbreak, forced Anthropic to shut down access for subscribers completely. It acknowledges minor vulnerabilities but says guardrails are being put in place. Ahead of Fable's release, it collaborated with the US government and the UK AI Security Institute (AISI) to address the government's security concerns. Anthropic argues the ban is unjustified, citing:
- No deployed model can ever be agnostic to a universal jailbreak.
- Fable's defense strategies provide resistance and are being continuously upgraded.
- Security agencies have not disclosed evidence of a non‑universal jailbreak in respect of Fable or Mythos.
- The only jailbreak instance was provided by Anthropic itself, which it suspects served as the basis for the directive.

In the backdrop of the Fable and Mythos ban on foreign subscribers, the need for sovereign IT infrastructure has become quite important. [India is the second-largest user base for Claude](https://www.hindustantimes.com/editorials/the-switch-india-doesn-t-hold-101781456580488.html). However, India does not have a homegrown sovereign LLM system like OpenAI or Anthropic. So, it does not hold an AI switch. In an earlier post on this blog, I had alluded to the need for a  [Sovereign digital framework](./Tech_Sovereignty.md) for a country as large as India, given its evolving AI-aided defense architecture. 

Anthropic's shutdown is not just a mere clash between users and the company—it exposes a deeper geopolitical power imbalance. As Sarvam [CEO Pratyush Kumar warns, access to AI without ownership leaves nations vulnerable](https://www.msn.com/en-in/money/topstories/dont-confuse-access-with-ownership-sarvam-ceo-on-what-anthropics-fable-ban-means-for-india/ar-AA25ARbM?gemSnapshotKey=GMFB619FB2-snapshot-6&uxmode=ruby&apiversion=v2&domshim=1&noservercache=1&noservertelemetry=1&batchservertelemetry=1&renderwebcomponents=1&wcseo=1). For India, the lesson is unmistakably clear: AI sovereignty is not about building another chatbot, but about securing control over defense, data, and digital infrastructure.
