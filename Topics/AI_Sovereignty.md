## Anthropic Shutdown & AI Sovereignty 

On Jun 12, 2026, the US Government issued directives to Anthropic to block all foreign subscribers from accessing its Fable 5 and Mythos 5. The ban triggered trolling of Infosys Chairman Nandan Nilekani on social media. Nilekani, in his recent address to shareholders, said that Indian IT companies should prioritize use cases rather than building yet another LLM. The substance of social media trolling is that the government is smarter than people like Nilekani. 

The US government, citing national security authorities, issued directives to Anthropic to prevent any foreign nationals or countries from accessing Fable 5 and Mythos 5 (https://www.anthropic.com/news/fable-mythos-access). Though Anthropic disagrees with the ban, it said it has no choice but to abruptly shut down Fable 5 and Mython 5 for all customers. No specific details regarding the security concern have been mentioned in the directive issued on June 12. 

One of the primary concerns is the potential for orchestrated cyberattacks using the tools Fable and Mythos. We have observed that Claued and other AI-assisted tools generated 1.5 million concurrent connections to the CBSE OSM portal within just two minutes. Although these attacks were smoke-screened as denial-of-service attacks, they were attempts to gain access to protected files. Fable 5 and Mythos 5 tools could lead to a huge number of unauthorized attempts to access sensitive information, especially since much of modern warfare is conducted from command and control centers over the Internet. 

Anthropic has worked closely with the US Military Intelligence and the government in the past. The dispute between Anthropic and the US government erupted in January 2026, leading to a government ban on Anthropic from government contracts. The company says that the latest government directive resulted from security authorities strongly suspecting a Fable 5 Jailbreak. The company says the minor vulnerabilities are known, and it is taking measures to put in place adequate guardrails against misuse. In the weeks leading up to the release of Fable, it worked with the US government and the UK AI Security Institute (AISI). Anthropic's view on disagreeing with the ban directive stems from two major facts.
 None of the deployed models can resist a perfect Jailbreak. 
Fables' established defense strategies provide resistance to Jailbreaks and are upgraded continuously. 
The company goes on to say that the government's security agencies have not provided any disclosure regarding a potential non-universal jailbreak. The company, on its own initiative, provided the government with an instance of a jailbreak, which it suspects served as the basis of the government directive. 

In the backdrop of the Fable and Mythos ban on foreign subscribers, the need for sovereign IT infrastructure has become increasingly important. India is the second-largest user base for Claude. India does not have a home to OpenAI or Anthropic. So, it does not hold an AI switch. Its entire AI investment budget over five years is 10,372 crore, spread across chipsets, datasets, skilling, and subsidies, which is roughly equivalent to the funding needed to build an LLM model](https://www.hindustantimes.com/editorials/the-switch-india-doesn-t-hold-101781456580488.html).  Sarvam CEO Pratyush Kumar says confusion between access with ownership and access without ownership can lead to disastrous consequences.  In an earlier post on this blog, I had alluded to the need for an IT Sovereign framework for a country as large as India, given the changing landscape of defense architecture.
