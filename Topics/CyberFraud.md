## Handling Cyber Fraud

[Blog Index](../index.md)

There is growing concern among senior citizens in India about various kinds of cyber fraud, which almost entirely snuffs out the urge and passion to live among victims. The type of financial fraud is carefully designed to exploit psychological issues and overall well-being (personal health, family stability, and children) that overwhelm senior people. Many of these people live relatively lonely lives and have to take care of themselves. A few samples of stories that make people fall for scammers are:  
- Instant and high-yield returns on investment schemes for senior citizens
- Family emergency known as "Grandparent" scams, saying grandchildren are in trouble with the law.
- Getting hospital admission for treatments
- Tech support guiding a digitally handicapped person to install rogue apps.
- SMS on lottery and rewards or investment from ousted corrupt emirs and rulers.
- Financial or banking requirements for KYC  updates.
- Online tickets, appointments for specialists, tax consultants, etc.
- Masquerading as a customer care executive, eliciting details of clients.
- Digital arrests by Federal law enforcement agencies.

Knowing incidents and stories that are generally woven around the psychosomatic state of mind helps, but it does not entirely make a person immune to traps. The end result of all such scams leads people to lose money. The endpoint of cybercrime is the victim's bank account. The digital fencing mechanisms of banks and financial institutions do help a lot. Most banks have additional layers of safety when withdrawing large sums from an account, but they fail to prevent an intelligent pattern of withdrawal tutored to victims by the scamsters. 

The Indian government has a portal for reporting cyber fraud. Citizens would like to know about the experience and ease of reporting cyber fraud through that channel. One issue that many victims face is the classic case of the "chicken and egg problem." If someone is a victim of cyber fraud, it most likely happens on a handheld device like a phone or iPad, via a cellular channel. Once the SIM has been cloned or a rogue app has hijacked the device, there is no way to use the SIM or the device. Interestingly, the Cyber Fraud portal insists on a phone number, details of the timing and channel on which the fraud occurred, Aadhaar card details, and many other personal details, including photographs or documents proving the fraud. Quite obviously, the documents can be extracted only through two-factor authentication to prevent any financial fraud. It implies that there is no way to know how to self-extract the documents. So, the only other way to obtain the records is to approach the bank or the relevant financial custodian. Hence, the victim loses the vital time between the event and its reporting, while the fraudster gains the advantage of time.

The other option is to approach the police, the worst handmaiden of the bureaucratic and governance setup. The police ask all the aforementioned details. If ordinary citizens were to collect these documents or ancillary proofs, it would take at least 6-8 hours of precious time. The window of time between the actual occurrence of the event and the reporting is more than sufficient for the fraudster to cover their tracks and vanish. So, what purpose does the cybercrime reporting portal serve? Now, a little truth about the police. The police have their own way of operating. The police department is highly corrupt and politicised to the extent that no law-abiding person can get reprieve from the police. Corrupt political bosses run most state governments, and the police are not only subservient to these bosses but also function as an extension of the political party in power. The police get rewarded by a share of the pie from what they gather for their political bosses. 

Now, about the victim's harassment after the fraud. In particular, in the case of unauthorized transactions, the victim must block all possible channels to prevent subsequent unauthorized transactions. It will mean electronic transactions must be stopped. Electronic transactions can be blocked using handheld devices. But if the device itself is hacked, then the victim should physically approach the custodian. That adds delay to the process. Sometimes, officials won't place a block unless the victim submits identity proofs such as Aadhaar and PAN. Therefore, the blocking process may not be as easy as it may sound. 

The second issue in the victim's harassment begins to unfold after the victim's device is sanitized completely. The bank will require a KYC update, and it may take up to 3 days to restore transaction rights. Most banks are migrating to app-based INB transactions. App-based INB is optimized for mobile devices and uses biometrics (fingerprints) or face ID for enhanced security. At the same time, web-based INB offers a broader user interface for more extended services. An app-based INB becomes useless on a hacked device. While web-based INB may work, operating without a sanitized phone could cause cascading effects of repeated scams.  The victim must undergo a series of interventions from the relevant branch of the bank before the resumption of services, as explained above. Both have advantages and disadvantages as shown in the table below.  


| Features | App-based INB | Web-based INB|
|--------|---------|--------|
|User experience | Smart design with touch friendly use | Text or Icon based switches for use|
| Updates frequency | High | Moderately low|
| Dependency | device dependent | Can be accessed from anywhere and any device|
| Connecity | Operates in low data connectivity | Rquires stable N/W connectivity|
| Performance | Optimized for device | Could time out when connection breaks|

The feature of frequent updates for app-based INB might be a hidden curse if the design flow is not very strictly verified. Typically, the app designer uses a hardware-software co-design approach that aligns with RBI's Digital Payment Security Controls and SEBI's Cyber Resilience Framework, enabling the use of device biometric sensors, SIM binding, and secure keystores. Cryptographic keys are stored in hardware, not in the app's storage. I don't know if the apps use AI-backed anomaly detections.

Most fraud in India occurs through SIM swapping and the installation of mobile malware. So, hardware-and-software co-design, including device binding, biometrics, and a secure keystore, is the best approach to prevent fraud even if the SIM is compromised. SBI YONO's hardware layer Android keystore/iOS secure enclave to store the cryptographic key. Its software layer uses end-to-end encryption for transactions. I am not sure whether SBI's YONO uses AI-aided fraud detection. HDFC is about to release its app-based INB. The bank uses its own Trusted Execution Environment (TEE, a sandbox environment) for secure PIN and biometric processing. The app is powered by AI-driven fraud detection logic. I am not aware of its performance yet.
