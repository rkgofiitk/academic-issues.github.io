## Handling Cyber Fraud Via Phones

[Blog Index](../index.md)

There is growing concern among senior citizens in India about various kinds of cyber fraud, which almost entirely snuffs out the urge and passion to live among victims. The type of financial fraud is carefully designed to exploit psychological issues and overall well-being (personal health, family stability, and children) that overwhelm senior people. Many of these people live relatively lonely lives and have to take care of themselves. A few samples of stories that make people fall for scammers are:  
- The phone is lost or stolen.
- Instant and high-yield returns on investment schemes for senior citizens.
- Family emergency known as "Grandparent" scams, saying grandchildren are in trouble with the law.
- Getting hospital admission for treatments.
- Tech support guiding a digitally handicapped person to install rogue apps.
- SMS on lottery and rewards or investment from ousted corrupt emirs and rulers.
- Financial or banking requirements for KYC  updates.
- Online tickets, appointments for specialists, tax consultants, etc.
- Masquerading as a customer care executive, eliciting details of clients.
- Digital arrests by Federal law enforcement agencies.

The endpoint of cybercrime is the victim's bank account. While banks deploy digital fencing and multi-layered security, scamsters often carefully train victims to bypass these safeguards. 

A stolen or lost phone may quickly escalate into serious cyber fraud. The police won't be very interested in the details of a missing phone, except for a general diary entry. No FIR is registered. I have faced it multiple times.
- Lucknow Airport:  CISF personnel courteously returned my phone.
- Hyderabad Airport: A passenger picked it up, delaying communication, but eventually returned it.
- Uber cab in Kolkata:  The driver denied possession, with the help of Police higher-ups, I traced and recovered the phone.  

The Indian government has a portal for reporting cyber fraud. Citizens would like to know about the experience and ease of reporting cyber fraud through that channel. One issue that many victims face is the classic case of the "chicken and egg problem." If someone is a victim of cyber fraud, it most likely happens on a handheld device like a phone or iPad, via a cellular channel. Once the SIM has been cloned or a rogue app has hijacked the device, there is no way to use the SIM or the device. Interestingly, the Cyber Fraud portal requires a phone number, details of the timing and channel of the fraud, Aadhaar card details, and many other personal details, including photographs or documents proving the fraud. Quite obviously, the documents can be extracted only through two-factor authentication to prevent any financial fraud. It implies that there is no way to know how to self-extract the documents. So, the only other way to obtain the records is to approach the bank or the relevant financial custodian. Hence, the victim loses the vital time between the event and its reporting, while the fraudster gains the advantage of time.

Approaching the police adds further hurdles. Victims must collect extensive documentation (in the way of ancillary proofs), often taking 6-8 hours of precious time, while the fraudster vanishes. Corruption and political interference have eroded trust in the police. Citizens think the police are just an extension of the political cadre base, like it was under the Colonial masters during the British Raj. 

Now, about the victim's harassment after the fraud. In particular, in the case of unauthorized transactions, the victim must block all possible channels to prevent subsequent unauthorized transactions. It will mean electronic transactions must be stopped. Electronic transactions can be blocked using handheld devices. But if the device itself is hacked, then the victim should physically approach the custodian. That adds delay to the process. Sometimes, officials won't place a block unless the victim submits identity proofs such as Aadhaar and PAN. Therefore, the blocking process may not be as easy as it may sound. 

The second issue in the victim's harassment begins to unfold after the victim's device is sanitized completely. The bank will require a KYC update, and it may take up to 3 days to restore transaction rights. Most banks are migrating to app-based INB transactions. App-based INB is optimized for mobile devices and uses biometrics (fingerprints) or face ID for enhanced security. At the same time, web-based INB offers a broader user interface for more extended services. An app-based INB becomes useless on a hacked device. While web-based INB may work, operating without a sanitized phone could lead to cascading effects from repeated scams. The victim must undergo a series of interventions with the relevant branch of the bank before services are resumed, as explained above.


The feature of frequent updates for app-based INB might be a hidden curse if the design flow is not very strictly verified. Typically, the app designer uses a hardware-software co-design approach that aligns with RBI's Digital Payment Security Controls and SEBI's Cyber Resilience Framework, enabling the use of device biometric sensors, SIM binding, and secure keystores. Cryptographic keys are stored in hardware, not in the app's storage. The advantages and disadvantages of both are shown in the table below.  


| Features | App-based INB | Web-based INB|
|--------|---------|--------|
|User experience | Smart design with touch friendly use | Text or Icon based switches for use|
| Updates frequency | High | Moderately low|
| Dependency | device dependent | Can be accessed from anywhere and any device|
| Connecity | Operates in low data connectivity | Rquires stable N/W connectivity|
| Performance | Optimized for device | Could time out when connection breaks|
| Security | Biometrics, SIM binding, secure enclave | Browser security, 2FA, session management |


India's most common fraud occurs through SIM swapping and the installation of mobile malware. So, hardware-and-software co-design, including device binding, biometrics, and a secure keystore, is the best defense. 
- SBI YONO uses Android keystore/iOS secure enclave with end-to-end encryption.
- HDFC App (upcoming): Employs Trusted Execution Environment (TEE) with AI-driven fraud detection.

India's banking ecosystem for fraud prevention is evolving rapidly. In the meantime, fraudsters and scammers are exploiting technical gaps and psychological vulnerabilities among potential victims. A stronger hardware-software co-design and AI-driven anomaly detection may finally make the banking system somewhat more impregnable for scammers. Meanwhile, vigilance and fast remedial actions remain the best defense against fraud until systemic changes tilt the balance in favor of citizens.
