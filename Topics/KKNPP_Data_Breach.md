## The KKNPP Data Breach: India's Nuclear Achilles Heel 

[Blog Index](../index.md)

India’s largest nuclear power project, KKNPP, suffered not just a data breach but a breach of trust in its ability to safeguard national security.
[Kundankulam nuclear power station (KKNPP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kudankulam_Nuclear_Power_Plant) is located in the Ramanathapuram district of Tamil Nadu. Its first two units, each with a capacity of 3000 MW, were built with a credit advance from Russia. The construction began in 2002 but was seriously hampered by several local agitations supported by environmental activists. The project is supposed to have six VVER-1000 reactors. 

The plant suffered a massive data theft by [Ransomware World Leaks](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/no-sensitive-data-leaked-government-refutes-reports-of-kudankulam-nuclear-plant-breach/articleshow/132443920.cms). Allegedly, the Group accessed 19k files linked to KKNPP, containing engineering documents, contractor details, and meeting records over almost a decade (2016 to 2025). It is surprising how the data theft remained undetected until now. It is unclear whether the Group accessed data in the cloud or hacked the KNPP computer system. Nearly 1.8 TB of data was stolen. What is alarming is that it contained sensitive data concerning layout design and many important pieces of information, such as the Balance of Plant (BoP) contract, Engineering, Procurement, and Construction (EPC), [NPCIL's](https://www.npcil.nic.in/) indicative drawings and technical specifications for the project prepared in consultations with OEMs, vendor details, and inspection reports. All data pertain to units 3 and 4, which are under construction. Although NPCIL confirmed that these data do not reveal nuclear security, the leaks reveal the extent of the problem in the third-party supply chain for running the KNPP. The data includes details on ventilation, the cooling system layout, and the floor plans for units 3 and 4. That is a really scary breach, because in the event of a serious conflict, the plant units can be targeted with precision by enemy forces. During operation Sindoor, many unverified social media chatters indicated that the IAF targeted an underground control and command center of Pakistan located in Nur Khan Airbase through a vent. Israel's take down of Qasim Sulemani, Hasan Nasarallah, along with his core commander group, Ali Khamenei, and the other Hamas and Hizbollah planners are cases of amazing precision strikes which would not have been possible without knowledge of associated data. So, it is really quite alarming that the Reliance Infrastructure Group could store data on [an unsecured cloud server](https://me.aiyu.co.in/blogs/kudankulam-nuclear-plant-data-breach-19000-files-leaked-by-world-leaks-ransomware). After downloading the data, the ransomware group apparently [demanded a ransom](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/kudankulam-nuclear-power-plant-data-leak-what-happened-and-what-we-know-explained/article71228955.ece). It threatened to publish the data if the demand was not met. Subsequently, it [published the data on the dark web ](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/kudankulam-nuclear-power-plant-data-leak-what-happened-and-what-we-know-explained/article71228955.ece) when the demand remained unpaid. 

Digressing a bit from the topic at hand, it is interesting to learn about the background and motives of the ransomware group World Data Leak Group. This Group was established in January 2025 through the rebranding of Hunters International, which was shut down in November 2021 by international law. It belongs to a [Russian-linked cybercriminal syndicate](https://www.quorumcyber.com/insights/from-hunters-to-hunted-quorum-cyber-exposes-new-hunters-international-malware/). The Group took responsibility for 134 attacks over seven months in 2024. World Leaks follows a double extortion model: 
- Demand a ransom and
- Leak the data if unpaid.

The operational camouflage of Hunters International was to provide RaaS (Ransomware as a Service) tools so that other cyber attackers with less sophisticated tools would be unable to carry out cyberattacks.  

World Leaks exhibits an uneven distribution of the victim footprint across several nations. The maximum number of victims, 91, are from the USA, and 7 each in India and Canada. Sector-wise distribution is even more interesting. Most victims are from healthcare, followed by manufacturing and industrial production, technology and electronics, and consumer service. However, attacks on strategic energy infrastructure signal a shift from corporate extortion to state-level destabilization and will probably become a recurrent tier in their victim selection. It is impossible to believe that the Group's notoriety was unknown to service providers and contractors. 

The Kudankulam data breach brings the supply chain's weak link into focus. [Reliance Infrastructure's](http://www.rinfra.com/) servers are hosted by [Yotta](https://yotta.com/ir/index.html?utm_source=copilot.com), which is part of the Hiranandani Group. It is a hyperscale data center and also a Meity-approved sovereign cloud platform. The NPCIL states that the data leak pertains to the Balance of Plant and is comparable to a conventional thermal plant. However, even the BoP data can be weaponized for sabotage and precision strikes. It raises a couple of serious threat issues.
- A precision strike could target a nearby nuclear reactor.
- Radiation from a reactor strike would not only affect flora and fauna but also create a permanent roadblock to energy security. 

Therefore, it reveals a dangerous gap between the official government views and those of independent experts.  

Although Thermal Power Plants also have strategic importance in the country's energy security, Nuclear Power Plants require the highest level of data security due to their vulnerabilities to radiation leaks. Some important initial recommendations for handling data security breaches are outlined below. The Atomic Regulatory Commission (ARC) should enforce 
- A Zero-trust architecture for all supply chain contractors.
- All sensitive data must remain on air-gapped, localized servers with strict protocols for access and admission control.
- The NPCIL, in collaboration with the ARC, must create a SoP for public disclosure of data breaches rather than leaving it to speculation.

IAEA already has a framework for nuclear cybersecurity. The ARC should study it to develop its protocols. The key features of the IAEA framework are as follows:
- Security has a graded approach, with each well-defined level having its own protocol in place. 
- Multiple levels prevent, detect, and respond to cyber attacks. -
- Cybersecurity is embedded in the overall security and safety management of the installed systems.
- Identification and protection of Sensitive Digital Assets, such as reactors, BoP systems, and monitoring networks.
- Strict access control regimes to mitigate insider threats. 

The Kudankulam breach is not a routine ransomware case—it is a stress test of India's nuclear resilience. By dismissing the leak as "non-sensitive," NPCIL risks underestimating adversaries who thrive on exploiting weak links. In a world where cyber intrusions can translate into precision strikes, India must harden its supply chain, enforce nuclear-grade cybersecurity across contractors, and embrace transparency. Nuclear power is meant to secure India's energy future, especially as the government seeks to strengthen energy security through fast-breeder reactors in partnership with the private sector.  Kudankulam has revealed how fragile that future remains if cybersecurity is treated as an afterthought.
