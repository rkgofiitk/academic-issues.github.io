## AI, Techlash, and the Rise of Soft Nuclear Power

[Blog Index](../index.md)

### Introduction

In his regular column "[Vardi's Insight" in CACM's November 2026](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3850023) issue, Moshe Vardi finds  Peggy Noonan's 2018 description of Silicon Valley executives appropriate for the spirit of times. Ms. Noonan is a weekly columnist for The Wall Street Journal. According to her, these executives are "moral Martians who operate on some weird new postmodern ethical wavelength." 

Vardi's article expands beyond Noonan's description, introducing the terms "cyberia" and "techlash."  Niall Ferguson, a historian at the Hoover Institution, describes "Cyberia" as a "dark, lawless space where malevolent actors reign." The word ["techlash"](https://itif.org/publications/2019/10/28/policymakers-guide-techlash/) is used by the Economist to describe a world of growing hostility towards tech giants. As an instance of emerging technological capabilities that raise governance and security concerns, the researchers from Stanford University and the Arc Institute demonstrated that they had successfully [synthesized a non-natural viral genome](https://www.georgetown.edu/news/ai-generated-viruses-bioengineering-ethics/).

Vardi refers to Noonan's latest column, "Pause AI for humanity's sake," published on September 10, 2026, in which she argues, "When something speeds up and gets out of control, it isn't alarmist to demand prudence and deliberation." The op-ed by the columnist Paul Tudor Jones in the Wall Street Journal, a day earlier, warns the US government that "AI May Become the Third Superpower."  The article further urges the US and Chinese Presidents to develop a joint strategy to confront the issue. 

The increasing prominence of AI-related risks in major business publications, such as the Wall Street Journal, reflects concerns among a segment of the US business community about the societal and geopolitical implications of these risks. It raises questions: 
- What AI-led developments are causing concerns?
- To what extent do these developments need broader public attention?

### AI-led Developments & Concerns
 
The public concerns about the rapid expansion of AI are across  the following domains:
- <strong>Opposition to data centers</strong>: Some political commentators say that opposition to data centers may have influenced voters in the [US midterm elections](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/red-counties-data-center-projects-swung-away-gop-midterms-analysis-finds). 
 - <strong>Labor market disruption</strong>: According to a Goldman Sachs estimate,  AI will eventually displace about 9% of the US workforce over a multi-year transition period.
- <strong>Bioterrorism</strong>: AI is adding to growing concerns. It may assist people with pathogen information in generating synthetic viral genomes. 
- <strong>Technofascism</strong>: Massive AI-enabled control systems may increase the ability of tech platforms and governments to exercise social and political influence.
  
Let us expand a bit on each of the above points. Training large AI models requires massive amounts of data, and the continuous running of inference workloads consumes a substantial amount of electricity, leading to carbon emissions and environmental sustainability concerns. A [Pew Research Center report](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/04/13/most-new-data-centers-in-the-us-are-coming-to-rural-areas/) lists the locations of data centers across different states in the USA. The locations are in exurban and rural corridors.

Goldman Sachs' estimate of labor market disruption pertains only to the USA. More detailed analysis of a survey of respondents from 37 countries is published in a [Pew Research Center report (September 17, 2026)](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2026/09/17/globally-more-people-expect-ai-to-cause-job-loss-than-growth/). According to the survey, about 46% of respondents believe AI adoption will lead to fewer jobs over the next two decades. The concern about job displacement is particularly pronounced among younger respondents (aged 18-34) from countries with higher GDP. These perceptions contribute to rising concerns about long-term security in a technologically automated labor market. 

Researchers and policymakers have expressed concerns that LLMs and specialized AI models may make it easier to overcome barriers in biological research on pathogens. This may increase [the risk of biosecurity breaches](https://globalbiodefense.com/2025/07/02/ai-bioterrorism-threat-biosecurity/) in the absence of a technical, institutional, and regulatory safety regime like IAEI. 

[Technofascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Techno-fascism) refers to  the "convergence of AI, big tech platforms, and corporate oligarchic power to establish authoritarian forms of governance." Algorithms could execute massive, AI-aided centralized surveillance and behavioral control, including individual profiling and behavioral patterns, in seconds. These developments have heightened the perception that increasingly sophisticated surveillance capabilities may enable more centralized forms of social and political control without robust democratic safeguards.

### Influences of Big Tech Firms

OpenAI has an annualized revenue growth of $50 billion, against losses of approximately $52 billion. The losses are mainly attributed to the requirements of massive infrastructure and model training costs. Despite substantial losses, it could still command investor confidence and is reported to have closed an [initial funding round of nearly 11 billion](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/open-ai-data-centers-election.html) and is pursuing an additional $20-30 billion in funding. 

Other big tech companies, Nvidia, Apple, Microsoft, and Alphabet, individually command market capitalization in the range of $ 3.5-5 trillion. [Grandview Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/artificial-intelligence-ai-market) estimates that the global AI market size is $ 540 billion in 2026. The combined market cap of the AI giants may exceed $20-25 trillion. Although market capitalization does not reflect the economic fundamentals, it indicates the extent of investor confidence and the potential influence within the financial market. Given the scale, resources, and technological reach of these firms,  they have substantial capacity to influence public debate, regulatory discussions, and policy development.

The debate over the AI push by big tech companies happens in multiple directions: 
- Technological optimism about economic growth, productivity, and the resolution of critical systemic bottlenecks and crises.
- Concerns about its influence on governance and security, and the disruption of the labor market, 
- Concentration of power within a few dominant big tech players.
  
Recently, OpenAI has reported advances in [machine-generated solutions to complex mathematical problems](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/). It has led to intense speculation about AI-driven disruptions across public, academic, and business discourse. The developments have intensified the discussion on how AI may reshape research innovation across disciplines.

Vardi's commentary may be interpreted as reflecting a broader shift in the AI debate. Many other influential commentators in the technology and innovation community are increasingly expressing concerns that were previously confined to AI critics. These concerns focus on the possibility that advanced digital technologies, including AI-driven surveillance systems, could alter the relationship between governments, corporations, and democratic institutions. The emergence of a technology backlash may encourage governments in countries such as the United States and China to reassess existing approaches to AI governance. Policymakers may seek to balance innovation objectives with concerns about competition, transparency, environmental sustainability, labor market disruption, and public accountability. 

AI workloads are highly energy-intensive, prompting major tech firms to explore and invest in nuclear power as a reliable source of large-scale, low-carbon electricity. At the same time, AI has the potential to significantly enhance operational efficiency, maintenance, and safety standards in nuclear power plants. While the relationship between AI and nuclear energy is mutually beneficial, the societal implications of widespread AI deployments are increasingly sparking intense public debates.  

In this sense, AI may be viewed as a form of "soft nuclear power": a general-purpose technology with significant economic and strategic value, substantial civilian applications, dual-use security implications, and growing geopolitical importance. Specialized physical resources, extensive infrastructure, and an international safety monitoring regime constrain the spread of nuclear technology. However, AI capabilities proliferate through software distribution, cloud platforms, and the open research ecosystem. Given its transformative potential and governance challenges, AI has sparked growing public debate over the creation of an effective policy framework to regulate its development.
