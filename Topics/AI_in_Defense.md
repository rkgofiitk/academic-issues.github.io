## AI in Surveillance & Warfare

[Blog Index](../index.md)

AI is being increasingly used for defense and surveillance. The success of recent military operations,  Israel's Hamas and Hezbollah wars, India's Sindoor Operations, and the US army operation to capture Mosul,  have used AI tools in some form or other. The ongoing Russia-Ukraine conflict has dragged on for over four years now because of the increasing unrestricted use of AI tools. Anthropic is in for [an extended fight](https://www.msn.com/en-in/news/other/anthropic-to-challenge-pentagon-in-court-hours-after-trump-orders-ban-on-ai-firm/ar-AA1XeXic?ocid=winp2fptaskbar&cvid=77f61e7200584edcc5c8e659984970ce&ei=39) with the US government for unrestricted access over deployment of [Claude](https://claude.ai/login) in surveillance and defense. Apparently, Anthropic CEO [Dario Amodei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dario_Amodei) is not averse to the Pentagon's use of Claude for military purposes. Pentagon has a live contract with Anthropic. AI appears to be a tactical soft nuke in conventional warfare, intelligence gathering, and prolonged military conflicts. 

It is nobody's concern that governments across the globe use sophisticated electronic tools for illegal surveillance, eavesdropping, and intelligence gathering. About 1.5 million IP-enabled cameras are deployed in public spaces in 15 major cities in India. On average, 83 cameras keep 24/7 watch over 1000 persons in Hyderabad (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_surveillance_in_India). Even second-tier cities like Indore compete with Delhi for the highest density of video and audio surveillance. Hyderabad, Delhi, and Chennai are among the most globally surveilled cities in the world. Over 91% of video cameras in India are concentrated in four cities: Hyderabad, Delhi, Chennai, and Indore. Interestingly, Bangaluru does not figure among these cities. The city police department has approximately half a million video cameras that it has successfully geotagged. Until recently, Bangaluru Police personnel were not wearing bodycams. Now it has been made mandatory. So, if the police cameras are not picking up your mannerisms (including when and how you pick your nose), then you must be either moving in an area under ill-deployed or non-functional cameras, or you have exceptional capabilities to move stealthily. If the latter is the case, you should share your expertise with HAL or establish a new startup to build a stealth aircraft. 

However, the question is, did AI change the whole game of mass surveillance or military conflicts? Let us focus on surveillance in the Indian context. Already, the government of India has biometric data for 1.34 billion people in the [Aadhaar database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aadhaar). Additionally, telecom companies have [KYC data](https://www.ckycindia.in/ckyc/index.php) for nearly 0.80 billion people. Any financial institution, such as a bank or an insurance company, holds vast amounts of data on private individuals. Almost 2.5 billion financial transactions occur every month. With such a high volume of data, it is possible to train AI models for every scenario and for multi-lingual Indic languages. The India [AI-Kosh repository](https://aikosh.indiaai.gov.in/home) contains 5722 datasets. [Bhasini](https://indiaai.gov.in/hub/indiaai-compute-capacity) (the government's own Indic-language translation mission) provides separate, rich datasets for speech and text. About 40% of Indian organizations hold 50-200 petabytes of industry data, of which 46% are in the public cloud. The national data center has 100 petabytes of data for AI model training and deployment. The Aadhaar-based Digi Yatra validation holds data for 0.11-0.14 billion air travelers. The initial plan was to auto-delete Personal Identification Information 24 hours after flight departure. However, some reports say the PII data is now retained for 30 days. It is just a few clicks that a focused surveillance staff would need to detect any pattern in a person's daily routine. However, inventing the click sequence requires solid human deductive capabilities. 

Therefore, the question is: what additional power would AI give? AI with computer vision capabilities will narrow the search and assist law enforcement in extracting a "black cat out of a coal cellar" without going on a wild goose chase. AI-assisted real-time facial recognition is used in public spaces. Vehicle license plate is tracked in real time through an AI-automated traffic violation and management system. Data analytics on crowd density and movement can predict a flash crowd in public spaces using AI-assisted, high-resolution computer vision tools. These tools may even flag an anomaly in crowd behavior patterns to assist the security apparatus. AI systems have an accuracy threshold, so conventional human management and oversight would be necessary. However, the exploration space for focused search becomes small.   While the positive side is understandable, the big brother watching becomes a reality. Governments thrive on incessant divisive discord among people. Engineering discord among people and groups by organizing gatherings is what political parties do day in and day out. However, using an AI model will make this more straightforward and more effective. The use of AI models in public surveillance could give the party in power an edge in managing public perception. It is likely to become a "soft tactical nuke" in demolishing a genuine opposition to the government.  

[Israel used AI in eliminating military and human targets in Gaza and Lebanon](https://futureuae.com/ar/Mainpage/Item/9735/blind-technology-israels-ai-deployment-in-gaza-and-lebanon-wars#:~:text=Israel%2C%20however%2C%20has%20employed%20AI,over%20to%20execute%20the%20mission). Gaza served as a testing arena for Israel's AI warfare. Similarly, India used an AI-capable air Defense system to intercept a swarm of drones and precision strikes during Operation Sindoor. Though AI is not a force multiplier, it is a catalyst for [Indian Military's year of technology absorption](https://thediplomat.com/2025/10/indias-use-of-artificial-intelligence-during-the-indo-pak-four-day-crisis/). DRDO is spearheading [Mission Sudarshan Chakra](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/mission-sudarshan-chakra/#:~:text=Mission%20Sudarshan%20Chakra%20is%20India's%20ambitious%20next%2Dgeneration,also%20enabling%20rapid%20and%20precise%20retaliatory%20strikes.) The target is to complete an integrated Iron-dome like air defense system for the country to detect, intercept and destroy incoming drones and missiles. The Mission's deadline is set for 2035. Geopolitical watchers say the Indian PM's visit to Israel is also part of this Mission to exchange technology and capabilities for mutual AI-enabled defense preparedness.
