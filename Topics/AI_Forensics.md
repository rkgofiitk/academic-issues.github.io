## AI Forensics and Investigative Criminology


Abstract

AI forensics has assumed increasing significance in investigative criminology and legal process. What is alarming is that AI tools are slowly penetrating the legal adjudication system. Given the background, this article examines the effectiveness and impacts of AI forensic tools in detecting violent crimes such as murders and terrorism beyond fraud, scams, industrial espionage, and surveillance. Not only are lawyers relying on AI tools to argue cases in courts, but judges also use AI tools to write judgments.

1. Background

A transition from relying on physical to digital forensics is a natural extension. Digital forensics is a well-known subject. In investigative criminology, presenting facts and supportive data with eye witness accounts, paper trails, and behavioral patterns have been used in securing prosecutorial success. However, there is an obvious barrier to the acceptance of AI forensics. AI forensics primarily relies on data for training. However, an inference engine is the heart of AI forensics. It uses a generative mechanism based on its training on past data to establish behavioral patterns. Therefore, the implementer's biases could lead to conclusions deviating far from actual occurrences.

2. Use of AI Forensic Tools

Many AI forensic software are available in the market. Evidence collection is an essential aspect of investigative criminology. It consists of the following:

- Audio and video evidence
- Surveillance and scene analysis
- Digital footprint and cyber evidence
- Forensic pathology, radiology, and biometrics
- Evidence management

AI audio and video evidence processing consists of speech-to-text transcriptions of interviews, interrogations, and surveillance videos scoured or retrieved from video cameras at the crime scene. Sometimes there is also a need for multilingual translation from regional languages or dialects to the language of courts and investigators. Furthermore, prosecutors may also suggest systematic annotations or tagging and timestamping. Surveillance and scene analysis have three distinct requirements: object identification, movements, and facial recognition. Uniqueness is a critical aspect of identification. Biometrics and supporting elements assist in establishing the uniqueness of identity, which is crucial to evidence authentication. Digital footprints and cyber evidence involve social media mining, emails, chat analysis for scam and deception, and coded exchanges. Evidence management deals with indexing for quick retrieval and predictive modeling for prosecutorial advantages.

Indian courts started using digital forensics after the enactment of the Information Technology Act, 2000. However, AI forensics is relatively new. A few notable Indian products for AI forensics are:

3. Evidentiary Appropriateness of AI

The fundamental question about the use of AI tools in the judiciary is centered on appropriateness. A safety constraint that governs the philosophy of jurisprudence, i.e., "no innocent person should ever be punished." In violent crimes, which attract the death penalty, the judge has to determine that the suspect is guilty beyond a reasonable doubt. AI forensics may lead to both false positives and false negatives. Therefore, the application of AI forensics to establish suspects' guilt may be questioned. Appeals and retrial requests will not only undermine the advantage of using AI forensics but also delay justice. Justice delayed is justice denied. Human oversight becomes relaxed when the prosecution relies heavily on AI forensics. So, instead of a reduction in pending cases, the load on courts may increase. On the other hand, the fundamental premise in using AI forensics is to reduce time and wrap up the investigation as fast as possible. Strict human oversight will ensure the appropriateness of using AI forensics.

However, one who may have observed the state of the judiciary in India will agree that our lower courts are ridden with corruption, and even many high court judges have delivered verdicts that defy evidence on the ground. Approximately 42 million cases are pending in district courts, 0.67 million in High Courts, and 0.087 million in the Supreme Court. There are three basic reasons for using AI forensics:

The district court decisions are critical, as most plaintiffs do not have the resources to approach appeals courts.
The district court goes through the details of the evidence and witness examination.
The corruption in the district courts is at the highest level in the judiciary.

Therefore, cleansing the lower judiciary is essential. The expectation is that AI forensics may at least raise the level of justice in lower courts. A few cases may reach appeals courts, where AI forensics may have led to a miscarriage of justice. At that time, careful human oversight may steer the conclusions of AI forensics.

4. Ethical Issues

There may be ethical issues surrounding using AI forensics in the legal process, especially for dispensing justice. However, the first issue is standardization and certification.

How do we ensure that the AI forensic tools are fair and blind in ascertaining the culpability of suspects in criminal investigations?
Has the implementor sufficiently and truthfully captured the prescribed standards and benchmarks for its results to be certifiable?

ISO/IEC has issued standards for testing AI forensics. However, these standards are relatively new and were issued between 2023 and 2025. ISO/IEC 42005, which deals with the AI system impacts, was announced only in 2025. It prescribes critical standards related to the admissibility of forensics. NIST (National Institute of Standards and Testing) of the USA has established computer forensic tool testing (CFTT) as a rigorous methodology for testing forensic software. It is basically a US-centric testing and certification procedure. I did not find any reference to determine whether the CFTT of NIST will apply to the Bharat Nyaya Samhita. I understand BIS is evaluating CFTT to ensure compliance with Bharat Nyaya Samhita. However, the question is: "Does the BIS have capabilities to do so?"

Beyond standards, testing, and certification, there is always a lingering issue of ethics in applying AI forensics to secure prosecutorial advantages against suspects in a criminal investigation.

Privacy and data protection
Biases and discrimination by profiling
Evidence integrity
Legal admissibility
Professional ethics and accountability

Suspects under a criminal investigation lose their privacy. Most AI forensic systems use pseudo-anonymization. Pseudo-anonymization replaces names with codes for evidence admissibility. However, pseudo-anonymization is reversible. Therefore, a suspect remains under a cloud for the rest of their life.

Criminal investigations often initiate with a hypothesis using extensive profiling and criminal records of the suspect. So, biases and discrimination are usually instrumental in criminal investigations. It is normal to expect that the investigators will steer AI forensics to progress towards reinforcing their hypothesis. Blaming AI forensics is not fair for outcomes in such eventualities; biased human oversight often creates a situation where the system may validate an illegal conclusion.

The problem could be very critical with caste, religion, and other racial groups. The absence of professional ethics in human oversight leads to biases in data selection and influences fairness in audits and inclusivity or exclusivity in modeling and behavioral pattern capture.

Implementors usually consult domain experts developing inference logics in AI systems. Proving that the specifications are sound and complete is tricky, especially in capturing inference logic. The two impediments are:

The quality of the domain expert's knowledge.
There may be a gap in knowledge transfer between the expert and the implementor.

A legal issue may have several interpretations. The implementor should work in close coordination with the domain expert and have clarity in drawing up all possible scenarios. Therefore, AI forensic tools cannot always be applied to every situation.

Computer software is always prone to hacking and cyber attacks. Imagining a scenario in which a compromised AI forensic system may give unexpected results is not entirely unthinkable.

5. Conclusion

Using AI forensics to detect financial fraud, scams, and cybercrimes is most promising. It is ideal for a tunnel vision of behavioral patterns and analysis of audit trails to discover the origin and traceability. However, AI forensics must be accompanied by competent human oversight to investigate violent crimes. However, AI forensics can significantly reduce cognitive biases in human investigators and quicken the pace of investigation.
