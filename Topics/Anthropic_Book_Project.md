## Training AI models with Knoweldge from Books and Digital Content

[Back to Index](../index.md)

Anthropic [bought print books worth million USD](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/24/anthropic-training/) in February 2024. Special service providers were employed to take down these books from their bindings, and cutting the contents into pages for digital scanning. The books were then discarded. Anthropic used the contents to train AI model using the knowledge from book. The question did Anthropic violate copyrignt laws? A group of three authors Andrea Bartz, Charles Graeber, and Kirk Wallace Johnson filed a law suit against Anthropic with the claim that it pirated knowledge from books to train AI models and benefit from that. However, a federal judge of US courts sided with Anthropic ruling that using legally purchased books for training purpose without the permission of authors is a fair use of the books. Human readers of the book uses their congnitive capabilities to internalize the acquire knowledge from books before being in a position to apply them in contextual settttings when required. I feel that an important conclusion from the court's ruling is that: 
- AI models have cognitive capabilities to internalize acquired knowledge from books as humans do.

Importantly, it places the AI models as entities comparable to human subjects. If so, can AI models be sued for being accomplices to acts of criminality if AI assisted models are eventually used for the criminal purpose? The judge is yet to addresse the issue of the outputs from AI models from acquired knowledge of the books. I assume that that the output from AI models would include the responses to the prompts. If the AI models do use the acquired knowledge from the books to output responses, then those responses come under outputs
of AI models. However, if AI models are likened to human entities in training on books, then I see no reason for the judge not to endorse the legality of the outputs from AI models. Only problem is that a human entity can handle a few prompts in a reasonable time due to bandwidth limitations. However, an AI model does not have any bandwidth limitations for responding to queries. It will be interesting to see if the judge applies a maximum threshold on use of AI models. If he does then it will create another set of problems in auditing and enforcing a reasonable threshold. 

The other question that the federal judge has not decided yet is the accusation that Anthropic has pirated millions of books from the Internet. I believe pirating books is a fairly simpler issue than training. However, if books are already available on the Internet and Anthropic has complied with the declared licensing agreements then pirating will probably not hold. It is already a big victory for Anthropic notwithstanding the two other pending issues before the court. I am sure many other AI companies like OpenAI, Google, etc., must also be closedly monitoring the outcome of verdict from Anthropic suing. 

In Indian context, the provision of copyright law is supposed to be quite robust. It gives automatic protection without the need for registration. It provides comprehensice coverage including creative multimedia and digital content. Provisions exists for both criminal and civil remedies against infringements. Whether an Indian court will consider training of programmating models as infringement is untested yet. However, Indian judicial apparateus is notoriously inadequate in enforcements of provisions of any Act.
