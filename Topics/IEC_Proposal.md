## Constitutionally Mandated Education Commission

[Blog Index](../index.md)

 **Abstract**

Of late, there have been ugly public spats between the governors and the state governments over the appointment of vice-chancellors.
State governments alone are not trying to wrest control of universities. The central government often appoints politically aligned "friendly" directors and vice-chancellors to IITs, AIIMs, IIMs, and central universities without diligence on their  qualifications, professional integrity, or experience. This article proposes creating a Constitutionally Mandated Education Commission  (CMEC) with plenary power to lay a robust framework for protecting higher educational institutions' operational and administrative autonomy. It could eliminate the unnecessary, avoidable political meddlings, progressively spreading tentacles to ruin the education system. 

 **1. Background**

The 42nd constitutional amendment of 1976 moved education, forest, wildlife, and justice to the concurrent list. There is no apparent rationale for the timing and necessity of the amendment, except that it happened during the emergency. It enabled the Federal government to exercise tighter control over the national institutions. Article 245 of the Constitution deals with the territorial extent of legislation by the Parliament and the state legislatures. In the case of a conflict, the central law prevails. Though the 42nd amendment reduced the states' autonomy, it allows implementation of a uniform national education policy (NEP), joint entrance tests for admissions (e.g., JEE, NEET), and a standardized curriculum across the nation. UGC was established in 1955. UGC could never implement its policies or advisories in state universities due to pushback. Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and Punjab governments argued that UGC norms cannot override existing state acts on appointments of VCs. The applicability of UGC's norms remained unresolved due to legal ambiguities. Instead of resolving the disputes, the courts added more confusion with conflicting judgments. UGC was made a statutory authority in 2018. However, the lack of plenary powers makes it ineffective against pushback from state governments. As a result, education governance suffered severely. Anchoring the creation of CMEC with a new article of the constitution will eliminate disputes arising out of existing state acts.

**2. Existing Governance Models**

We came across four existing governance models, namely:
- Wissenschaftsrat (Germany)
- Global Education Commission (UN)
- CHE (South Africa)
- UGC (India)
  
The last two represent administrative models created under the respective education ministries by statutory provisions. Wissenschaftsrat is an advisory body with federal recognition, while GEC, formally known as the International Commission on Financing Global Education Opportunity, is a global initiative under the UN. GEC influences participating nations via recommendations, reports, and feedback. 

The utility of UGC is limited. It is a statutory body created by an Act of Parliament that lacks plenary power and is often contested by states. The intention is to safeguard the operations of the universities and other institutions of higher learning from needless, unhinged interference by politicians. Let us analyze the consequences of political interference.  

Though the media often talks about NIRF, QS and THE rankings of institutions, they don't reflect employers' perception. Employer perception accurately measures an institution's ranking and the direct consequence of political interference in an institution.  For example, Anna University and Amity rank alongside IITs. The employers' perception score for IIT Bombay graduates is 96.7, whereas the scores are between 30 and 45 for Amity and Anna University, respectively. Similarly, though JU is considered the topmost state engineering college, employers' perception scores for JU graduates range between 37 and 41. Citizens helplessly witness politicians ruin the education system. It has manifested in disastrous consequences like:
- Unemployable graduates
- Paralysis of education governance
- Erosion of academic freedom
- Poor research and innovation
- Decoupling of industry and academia
  
If the proposal for setting up a CMEC anchored under a new Article of the Constitution is adopted, it will give plenary power to the commission. The proposal is unique without a parallel anywhere in the world. 

**3. The Proposal**

The core of our proposal is to establish a Constitutionally Mandated Education Commission (CMEC) in line with the Election Commission of India (ECI). With plenary power, the commission can effectively control educational institutions nationwide. It would be a unique governance model with no parallel anywhere globally. It has become necessary due to constant political meddling in the country's higher education system. 

The CMEC will have jurisdiction over all public and private institutions of higher learning. The CMEC will:

- Ensure independent, merit-based evaluations of candidates for appointments of Directors and Vice-Chancellors
- Separate academic and administrative authority via a closely-coupled Provost–Director model
- Reconstitute university Senates as binding academic bodies
- Enforce cryptographic audit trails for transparency and accountability
- Protect institutions from political sabotage and ideological coercion
- Preserve federal balance through state-level cells and opt-in incentives

**4. Appointment of VCs and Directors**

Appointment of directors of CFTIs and vice-chancellors of universities is a key issue in university governance. We have helplessly watched how the politicians usurp control in proxy by appointing pliable "friendly" directors or vice-chancellors. In an [earlier article](https://github.com/rkgofiitk/academic-issues.github.io/blob/gh-pages/universityChiefExecutive.md), I discussed the appointment of directors at IITs. Intense lobbying, even by top-rated educators and decorated researchers, is sickening. It exhibits the decay and rot that has set in the educational landscape across the nation.

The appointment committee under the proposed commission would be a statutory body. It will function in the UPSC style. The VC or the Director would be the institution's chief executive and an officer for external liaison. The  Senate or the Syndicate of the institution will function as the academic authority. The Chairperson of the apex academic authority will be the Provost. The Provost and Chief Executive (Director/VC) will represent a closely-coupled system of educational governance. Such a governance model ensures a proper check and balance. The Provost will represent the aspirations of students, faculty, and key academic stakeholders.
On the other hand, the VC or the Director will guide the institution to society's requirements, expectations, financial resources generation, and global standing. The Provost will be a co-opted member of the commission's appointment committee. If the Provost is a candidate, the seniormost member of the concerned Senate or Syndicate will be chosen as the co-opted member. 

**5. The Role of the Senate** 

The Senate of each institution is the apex academic body with representation from the faculty, students, and other key academic entities. Unfortunately, the Senates are now made dysfunctional. Bifurcating the administrative responsibilities of Provost and Director/VC will insulate academic decision making from unnecessary external interference. The CMEC must ensure the reorganization of the Senate through a policy statement.

**6. Transparency, Accountability, and Audits**

The commission should establish a provable audit trail to highlight its efforts to bring transparency and accountability to each institution. This can be made possible by adopting a cryptographic hash-based audit system. The commission should maintain an official public participation channel and continuously engage the society to improve its operations and workflow. There will still be a dependence on the government for fund allocations. However, the Constitutional mandate will guarantee the flow of funds. The education and healthcare cess collected via income tax could partially fund the commission. The government could rework GST share allocations and choose to eliminate the education cess from income tax. Furthermore, the government could persuade the industries to earmark a part of CSR funding for commissions annual budget outlay.

 **7. State-level Participation**                     

The constitution of the commission can be in line with the ECI. Therefore, it can include state-level participation by forming state cells. It will reduce the burden on the state's Directorate of Higher Education and make it more efficient. The commission should identify eminent educationists from each state to create state-level cells.

**8. Expected Outcomes**

The first and foremost outcome of CMEC oversight is eliminating campus politics and the meddling of political parties in university affairs across the nation. CMEC will ensure that each institution is administered efficiently and stakeholders can participate effectively in academic decision-making. It will increase public confidence in the government's intentions of eliminating politics from campuses. The overall global ranking of institutions will also increase. India's academic sovereignty will be a model for others to emulate, including the developed nations. Institutions can directly approach constitutional courts to resolve disputes arising from their decision. The overall structure will effectively provide resilience against political sabotage without judicial scrutiny. The Whistleblower Protection Act would ensure that any incursion into academic freedom or financial embezzlement by university administrations will be prosecuted, and perpetrators receive adequate punishments.  In summary, we can view the outcome under the following heads:
- Structural Improvements
- Academic and Operational Improvements
- Legal and Jurisdictional Resilience 
- Global Positional Improvements
