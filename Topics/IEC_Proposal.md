## Independent Education Commission of India 

[Blog Index](../index.md)

 **Abstract**

Of late, there has been a lot of conflict between the governors and the state governments in appointing vice-chancellors to the state universities. 
The federal government is accused of recruiting "friendly" directors and vice-chancellors to IITs, AIIMs, IIMs, and central universities without due diligence into their qualifications and experience. In this article, we propose that a Constitutionally Mandated Education Commission  (CMEC) could eliminate the unnecessary, avoidable meddlings in the education system. 

 **1. Background**

The 42nd constitutional amendment of 1976 moved education, forest, wildlife, and justice to the concurrent list. There is no apparent rationale for the timing and necessity of the amendment, except that it happened during the emergency. It enabled the Federal government to exercise tighter control over the national institutions. Though the amendment reduced the states' autonomy, it allows the implementation of uniform education administration policies such as the national education policy (NEP), joint entrance tests for admissions, and a standardized curriculum across the nation. UGC was established in 1955. However, due to pushback from state governments, UGC could never implement its policies or advisories in state universities. Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and Punjab governments argued that UGC  norms cannot override existing state acts on appointments of VCs. The applicability of UGC's norms remained unresolved due to legal ambiguities. The courts added more confusion instead of resolving the disputes via their conflicting judgments. UGC was made a statutory authority in 2018. However, the lack of plenary powers makes it ineffective against pushback from state governments. As a result, education governance suffered severely. Article 245 of the Constitution deals with the territorial extent of legislation by the Parliament and the state legislatures. In the case of a conflict, the central law will prevail. Anchoring the creation of CMEC with a new article of the constitution will eliminate disputes arising out of existing state acts.

**2. Existing Governance Models**

We came across four existing governance models, namely:
- Wissenschaftsrat (Germany)
- Global Education Commission (UN)
- CHE (South Africa)
- UGC (India)
  
The last two represent administrative models created under the respective education ministries by statutory provisions. Wissenschaftsrat is an advisory body with federal recognition, while GEC, formally known as the International Commission on Financing Global Education Opportunity, is a global initiative under the UN. GEC influences participating nations via recommendations, reports, and feedback. 

The utility of UGC is limited. It is a statutory body created by an Act of Parliament that lacks plenary power and is often contested by states. The intention is to safeguard the operations of the universities and other institutions of higher learning from needless, unhinged interference by politicians. Though the media often talks about QS and THE rankings of institutions, they don't reflect employers' perception. For example, Anna University and Amity rank alongside IITs. In reality, the employers' perception represents that IIT Bombay graduates score 96.7, whereas Amity and Anna University scores are between 30 and 45. JU is considered the topmost state engineering college. The employers' perception score for JU graduates ranges between 37 and 41. It is a measure of political interference in the institution. Citizens helplessly witness politicians ruin the education system. It has led to disastrous consequences like:
- Unemployable graduates
- Governance Paralysis
- Erosion of academic freedom
- Poor research and innovation
- Decoupling of industry and academia
  
If the proposal for setting up a CMEC anchored under a new Article of the Constitution is adopted, it will give plenary power to the agency. The proposal is unique as there is no parallel anywhere in the world. 

**3. The Proposal**

The core of our proposal is to establish a Constitutionally Mandated Education Commission (CMEC) in line with the Election Commission of India (ECI). It will have plenary power to exercise its control over educational institutions nationwide. It would be a unique governance model with no parallel anywhere globally. It has become necessary due to constant political meddling in the country's higher education system. 

The CMEC will have jurisdiction over all public and private institutions of higher learning. The CMEC will:

- Ensure independent, merit-based evaluations of candidates for appointments of Directors and Vice-Chancellors
- Separate academic and administrative authority via a closely-coupled Provost–Director model
- Reconstitute university Senates as binding academic bodies
- Enforce cryptographic audit trails for transparency and accountability
- Protect institutions from political sabotage and ideological coercion
- Preserve federal balance through state-level cells and opt-in incentives

**4. Appointment of VCs and Directors**

Appointment of directors of CFTIs and vice-chancellors of universities is a key issue in university governance. We have helplessly watched how the politicians usurp control in proxy by appointing pliable "friendly" directors or vice-chancellors. In an [earlier article](https://github.com/rkgofiitk/academic-issues.github.io/blob/gh-pages/universityChiefExecutive.md), I discussed the appointment of directors at IITs. Intense lobbying, even by top-rated educators, is sickening. It exhibits the decay and rot that has set in the educational landscape across the nation.

The appointment committee under the proposed commission would be a statutory body. It will function in the UPSC style. The VC or the Director would be the institution's chief executive and an officer for external liaison. The  Senate or the Syndicate of the institution will function as the academic authority. The Chairperson of the apex academic authority will be the Provost. The Provost and Chief Executive (Director/VC) will represent a closely-coupled system of educational governance. Such a close-coupled governance model ensures a proper check and balance. The Provost will represent the aspirations of students and faculty.
On the other hand, the VC or the Director will guide the institution to society's requirements, expectations, and global standing. The Provost will be co-opted as a commission's appointment committee member. If the Provost is a candidate, another member of the concerned Senate or Syndicate will be taken as a co-opted member. 

**5. The Role of the Senate** 

The Senate of each institution is the apex academic body with representation from the faculty and students. Unfortunately, the Senates are now made dysfunctional. Bifurcating the academic administration from executives will make it possible to ensure the independence of academic decisions from unnecessary external interference. The CMEC must ensure the reorganization of the Senate through a policy statement.

**6. Transparency, Accountability, and Audits**

The commission should establish a provable audit trail to highlight its efforts to bring transparency and accountability to each institution. This can be made possible by adopting a cryptographic hash-based audit system. The commission should maintain an official channel for public participation and engage them in continuously improving its workflows. There will still be a dependence on the government to fund its activities. However, the central government charges an education and healthcare cess with income tax. The government can use the GST route and eliminate the education cess from income tax. Furthermore, the government can persuade industries to earmark some CSR funding to finance the commission. 

 **7. State-level Participation**                     

The constitution of the commission can be in line with the ECI. Therefore, it can include state-level participation through state cells of the commission. It will reduce the burden on the state's Directorate of Higher Education and make it more efficient. The commission should identify eminent educationists from each state to create state-level cells.
