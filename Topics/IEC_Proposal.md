##  Independent Education Commission of India 

[Blog Index](../index.md)

 **Abstract**

Of late, there have been a lot of conflicts between the governors and the state governments, which are severely affecting universities and other institutions of higher learning. Even the central government is mishandling the institutions of higher learning by orchestrating the selection of "friendly heads" to IITs, AIIMs, IIMs, and central universities without due diligence into their qualification and experience. In this article, we propose that an independent, constitutionally mandated education commission could eliminate the unnecessary, avoidable meddlings in the education system. 

 **1. Background**

The 42nd constitutional amendment of 1976 moved education, forest, wildlife, and justice to the concurrent list. We don't find any rationale for the timing and necessity of the amendment, except that it happened during the emergency. It enabled the Federal government to exercise tighter control over the national institutions. Indira Gandhi may have considered institutions of higher learning and the judiciary a threat to her continuation as Prime Minister. Though it reduced the states' autonomy, the amendment helped implement uniform education administration policies such as the national education policy (NEP), joint entrance tests for admissions, and a standardized curriculum across India. UGC was established in 1955 by the first education minister of the Nehru cabinet. However, UGC could never implement its policy advisories to state universities due to pushback from state governments such as Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and Punjab, who argue that UGC  norms on appointments cannot override existing state acts. The issue of UGC's norms applicability remained unresolved due to legal ambiguities, and the courts created more confusion by conflicting judgments. UGC was made a statutory authority in 2018. However, the lack of plenary powers made it ineffective due to pushback from state governments. As a result, education governance suffered severely, and a leadership vacuum remained a permanent issue across all Indian institutions. 

**2. The Core Proposal**

The core of our proposal is to establish a Constitutionally Mandated Education Commission (CMEC) in line with the Election Commission of India (ECI). It will have plenary power to exercise its control over educational institutions nationwide. The proposed governance model is unique; no parallel exists anywhere globally. The comparison matrix shows the uniqueness of our core proposal. We based our comparison on four different governance models:
- Wissenschaftsrat (Germany)
- Global Education Commission (UN)
- CHE (South Africa)
- UGC (India)
  
The last two governance models are statutory bodies under the respective education ministries. Wissenschaftsrat is an advisory body with federal recognition, while GEC is a global initiative under the UN. So, the proposal for setting up a CMEC under Article 315 of the Constitution is unique. The intention is to safeguard it from needless, unhinged meddling by politicians. We are helplessly witnessing politicians ruin the education system. It has led to disastrous consequences like:
- A void in the availability of skills 
- Unemployable educated youths who become fodder to political parties of all hues.

The CMEC will have jurisdiction over all public and private universities and the institutions of higher learning that now come under the Ministry of Education of the federal and state governments. We propose a separate UPSC-style appointment committee under the commission for appointments of directors and vice-chancellors. We are proposing a firewall against politicization, opacity, and institutional decay. 

**3. Appointment of Heads**

Appointment of directors and vice-chancellors for CFTIs and universities is a key issue. We have helplessly watched how the politicians usurp control in proxy through appointing a director or a vice-chancellor. In an [earlier article](../universityChiefExecutive.md), we discussed the appointment of directors at IITs. We intend to create a statutory appointment committee under the commission. It will function in the UPSC style. However, we propose to create a separate position of provost as the head of the academic bodies, like the Senate and the Syndicate. The director, or the vice-chancellor, will closely associate with the provosts, and the two function as a check and balance to ensure that an institution's internal dynamics are factored transparently in its public perceptions. The provost will be co-opted as a commission's appointment committee member. If the provost is a candidate, another member of the concerned Senate or Syndicate will be taken as a co-opted member.
