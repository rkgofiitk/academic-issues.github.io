## AI Platform for Military Operations, National Security, and Governance

[Blog Index](../index.md)

This post explores the transformative potential of AI-based Platforms for military operations, national security, and governance oversight. The [Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon) has acquired two key products, Gotham and Foundry, from [Palantir Technology Inc](https://www.palantir.com/). It is actively using them for detecting financial fraud, internal security and surveillance, defense, and battlefield use. 

The word ["Palantiri"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palant%C3%ADr) refers to an indestructible stone that has a very interesting connotation with capabilities to allow users to communicate and see things beyond even technologically augmented extra-sensory perception. However, it comes with a risk of manipulation, as users may perceive different and conflicting visions. It is a magical crystal ball. Therefore, it is not entirely surprising to learn that a software platform named after the proverbial crystal ball specializes in intelligence, defense, law enforcement, and counter-terrorism. 

The company Palantir, founded by Peter Thiel, Joe Lonsdale, and Nathan Gettings, manages massive amounts of data to provide far-reaching insights into complex systems. The system cannot be precisely categorized as an ERP, a data warehouse, a business intelligence system, or middleware. It offers a hybrid middleware to combine all four. Palantir offers two flagship products: (i) Gotham and (ii) Foundry. The Foundry works like an operating system that collects data, ties it together, manipulates it, and integrates it across multiple sources. Gotham uses AI techniques (machine learning) to leverage the data captured by Foundry for decision-making and analysis. [Gotham is named after the hometown of Batman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotham_City), plagued by crime, corruption, and immoral activities. The metaphor used by Palantir symbolically conveys dealing with complex interaction of dense data repositories of an unpredictable urban landscape. 

A nation's governance faces challenges in managing data across agencies, jurisdictions, and formats. Gotham provides tools for data fusion across agencies, enabling the building of analytics to project patterns and predict eventualities. Let us understand why it is critical to acquire a data fusion platform. A fragmented data ecosystem is inherent across government agencies, which work in silos and lack an effective handshaking mechanism. There are several reasons for the lack of cooperation among agencies. However, the key concern is the issue of credit sharing. In counter-terrorism and disaster monitoring, real-time data and intelligence gathering are very crucial. Legal and ethical standards are essential for effective governance, as well as audit trails to support and corroborate decision-making. India's upcoming [national data exchange](https://www.data.gov.in/national-data-platform) promises to be a unified interoperable market place for both government and private stakeholders. So far there is only health and family welfare ministry have a sizable presence on NDP. 

How could a data fusion and an analytic platform enhance governance? The fusion of real-time data is critical for governance oversight, operational clarity, counter-terrorism, and military operations. Data fusion reduces data volume and provides an effective filtering mechanism to remove noise. It assists in accessing audit trails, enabling data minimization, and allowing agencies to make effective decisions that comply with legal and ethical standards, thereby avoiding jurisdictional overreach.

China has developed multiple Big Data surveillance platforms that rival Gotham. Among these, Skynet, Sharpeyes, and IJOP (Integrated Joint Operations Platform) are notable products that offer tools for intelligence gathering, domestic security, and population monitoring. 

Unfortunately, the initiatives through the government of India have led to fragmented systems across law enforcement, intelligence gathering, and governance. Except forsmall piece about the [US government mapping data to the Palantir system](https://www.strategicstudyindia.com/2025/09/palantir-is-mapping-government-data.html), there is hardly any literature on strategic studies from an Indian perspective in the public domain.  There are tools such as CCTNS, NATGRID, and ICCC under the Smart City Mission. The primary focus of  CCTNS is police digitization. CCTNS does not provide real-time analytics and lacks AI capabilities. NATGRID combines data from Banks, airlines, and telecoms for intelligence gathering concerning counter-terrorism. However, it cannot provide real-time data analytics. The primary focus of ICCC is real-time data on city traffic, vehicle license plate analysis, utilities, and public safety. Tech Mahindra and L&T are involved in ICCC.  

Private sector initiatives have primarily focused on financial and enterprise system analytics. [Fractal Analytics](https://fractal.ai/), [Mu Sigma](https://www.mu-sigma.com/), and [SAS India](https://www.sas.com/en_in/home.html), [TCS](https://www.tcs.com/), [Infosys](https://www.infosys.com/) are active in these domains. Fractal Analytics uses AI and decision intelligence for enterprises. Mu Sigma offers data science services for business intelligence. SAS India provides analytics solutions related to national security. TCS and Infosys implement SAS in India, but neither provides a centralized platform. 

TCS, Tech Mahindra, L&T, Infosys, HCL, Wipro, and many other Indian companies that specialize in Enterprise software have the intrinsic capabilities to build innovative indigenous products, rivaling those of Gotham or Foundry. In the fintech sector, they certainly possess the abilities and may already have the tools to rival Plantir Foundry. However, I did not find any reference to a Gotham-like platform is primarily targeted at operations that typically pertain to GoI operations. Therefore, unless the government supports such a project, I doubt the companies would be willing to commit resources to building a good platform. I will refrain from voicing a suspicion of systematic sabotage, even if some in the government are willing to execute such a project. The problem arises from the fact that India is a "noisy democracy", where people hold diverse views and struggle to unite even in defense and military operations connected with the country's sovereignty. A {recent remark of the LoP](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/10-control-the-army-rahul-gandhis-caste-claim-at-bihar-rally-says-90-indians-nowhere-to-be-found/articleshow/125088800.cms) is a pointer to the climate of fragmentation that exists in India.
