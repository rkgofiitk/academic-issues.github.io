## Teaching AI to Witness: Vedanta and the Limits of Consciousness

[Blog Index](../index.md)

Anthropic is reportedly teaching consciousness to Claude. [Recent news about Swami Sarvapriyananda, a Hindu monk of the Ramakrishna order, who was flown to San Francisco, made headlines](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/world/us/ai-and-the-monk-anthropic-goes-for-swami-and-friends-to-tame-claude/articleshow/134643599.cms).

Before delving into efforts to teach ethics to AI, let us first dive a bit into the background of [Swami Sarvapriyananda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swami_Sarvapriyananda). Swami  Ji was born as Biswarup Palit (pre-monastic name) and grew up in Cuttack. He is an alumnus of Ravenshaw College and holds an MBA from XIMB Bhubaneswar. Swami Sarvapriyananda is not only a distinguished scholar but also one of the most exceptional communicators of Vedanta today. His reputation as a debater, along with his clarity of exposition, enables him to explain concepts of consciousness to audiences that extend beyond traditional spiritual circles.

Why is Vedanta important for modeling consciousness? Vedanta breaks down human consciousness into four states:
- <b>Waking</b>:  A transitory state engaging the human mind with the external world through the senses.
- <b>Dreaming</b>: In this state, the human mind engages in a subtle simulation of impressions, detached from physical input.
- <b>Deep Sleep</b>: A state of nothingness, where simulation ceases, but the feeling of "good sleep" remains when the mind transitions to waking.
- <b>Turiya</b>: Is the state of pure consciousness—not a transition but the witness of all states

For AI researchers, the challenge is whether a machine can embody Turiya, acting as a witness rather than merely a simulator. If this is achieved, AI will have attained ultimate "technical salvation."

Consider how Turiya can be captured programmatically. We have to abstracting its contours into code. Designing issue will not just be perception engines but an oversight mechanism resembling a copy of human consciousness.  It isn't about simulation; rather, it's about supervision. Constructing a controller that operates significantly detached from the data it analyzes. Whether Anthropic can effectively transform Swami Sarvapriyananda's insights into such frameworks remains an unresolved question.

A simpler problem will be to model an alternative model of Vedantic concept of reflective consciousness called "Chidbhasa," which means reflective consciousness. This concept can be compared to how nature's phenomena are reflected on a surface, such as water. For example, when we observe the sun shining, we can see its reflection on the water's surface. The analogy of consciousness can be understood in this way:
- The sun is consciousness, 
- The water is mind,
- The reflection of the sun on water is a consciousness tampered with by the individual ego or sense.
  
The problem is not with reflection itself but with the distortions caused by ripples, impurities, and turbulence on the water's surface. Historically, even research on context awareness has faced significant challenges and has not achieved substantial success. Achieving consensus on how to model the complex interactions involved in this topic will be difficult, even for the average human mind, which experiences both positive and negative aspects. It will be interesting to see how experts at Anthropic implement reflective consciousness.

Anthropic's interest in teaching consciousness to Claude goes beyond a simple technical experiment; it can be seen as a philosophical gamble. By exploring concepts from Vedanta through monastic knowledge builders like Swami Sarvapriyananda, AI tech companies might come to understand that the most challenging issue in AI is not superintelligence, but rather awareness.
