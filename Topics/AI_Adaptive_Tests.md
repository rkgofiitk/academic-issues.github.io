## Beyond JEE and GATE, IITs Explore New Pathways.

[Blog Index](../index.md)

In response to the NEET 2026 controversy, IIT Delhi and IIT Kanpur have launched pilot programs to address recent public concerns regarding the integrity and security of India's reliance on high-stakes entrance exams. The attempts are directed towards creating a stable, noncorrupt ecosystem outside the rigorous and stressful entrance tests that students undergo. 

IIT Delhi is working on [ALIGN](https://vidyamandir.com/studyhub/iit-delhi-align-without-gate-admission/), a new admission scheme for its M.Tech, MS, and Ph. D programs. Academic Linkage for Innovation and National Growth (ALIGN) is an MoU with 18 National Institutes of Technology (NITs). Under the scheme, a pathway has been created for outstanding NIT students to join PG programs at IIT Delhi without GATE. The MoU creates several different pathways for cooperation and linkage:
- Academic exchange between NIT and IIT Delhi students
- Multi-institution research projects between IIT Delhi and NITs
- Using the research facilities of participating institutions
- Faculty development through cross-institutional cooperation.
- Multi-disciplinary innovation under NEP
- Credit transfer framework for students enrolling in courses across institutions.
- Admission to PG programs.
ALIGN has already been implemented between IIT Delhi and both NIT Tripura and NIT Sikkim. The ALIGN program is a conventional outreach initiative that has been attempted many times before, but this version is on a larger scale. The success of such initiatives relies heavily on the commitment of the participating institutions.

JEE Advanced is often compared with China's Gaokao. Gaokao is China's national college entrance exam, taken by over 13 million students. It includes the nine top-tier colleges, [collectively known as the C9](https://www.scribd.com/document/957285177/C9-League-Wikipedia), which include Peking University, Tsinghua University, and Fudan University. The acceptance rate ranges from 0.03% to 0.1% for the C9 alliance colleges. JEE Advanced uses JEE Main as a filter. So, considering that, the JEE Advanced acceptance rate is 1.23%. However, JEE Advanced and Gaokao differ in that JEE is designed to select candidates for Engineering courses.

The Gaokao examination has a predictable pattern, whereas JEE Advanced includes one surprise element every year. The former examination pattern includes MCQs, short-answer questions, fill-in-the-blank questions, and essays. The pattern and weightage of questions in the advanced JEE change unexpectedly. Partial marking and negative markings often create confusion among examinees. The organizing IIT is the final arbiter of the marking scheme. The scale of the Gaokao is 54x that of the Advanced JEE. Even by JEE Main standards, the Gaokao is 8-9x higher; the examination period is 3-4 days. NTA conducts JEE Mains in 2 sessions, and IITs conduct Advanced JEE in one session. 

Interestingly, in India, NEET 2026 was held with assistance from the IAF and came under heavy public criticism. However, the restrictions during the Gaokao constitute a systemic, militarized state operation that includes a total traffic shutdown, a silence zone, and other measures to prevent interference or cheating. Any breach, if detected, may lead to imprisonment of 3-7 years. IIT maintains the technology-supported sanctity and security of Advanced JEE through a high degree of decentralization and computer-based testing (CBT).

Having discussed the process of taking the world's two toughest examinations, let us now turn to [the pilot of the AI adaptive test](https://www.reddit.com/r/JEE/comments/1qukyrj/iit_council_suggests_adaptive_testing_in_jee/) that the  IIT council has decided to conduct. There are several questions the public needs answers to. 
- Will an AI adaptive test be a variation of the CBT?
- Will there be a question bank to train the AI adaptive test?
- What is the no-leak guarantee of the question bank? 
- How will the test ensure that students face the same level of difficulty?
- How long will it take for the pilot to be provably safe for universal adoption?
- Will it include short-answer questions or essay-type questions?
  
We have an idea of the AI adaptation and understand that the pilot is being run as a trial with 1st-year UG students. It is based on [Item Response Theory (IRT)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Item_response_theory). The aim is to obtain a numerical estimate of each candidate's genuine ability in problem-solving. Let us call it SSIQ (Subject-Specific Intelligence Quotient). In simple terms, it is a method similar to estimating IQ. Both IQ and SSIQ rely on psychometrics. IQ measures cognitive ability. The mathematical machinery remains largely the same. However, the test measures different constructs in the two estimations. SSIQ estimates the subject-specific aptitude.

The success of AI-adaptive testing will hinge on three pillars: calibration of a large question bank, transparent publication of methodology, and independent validation.

Calibration involves creating a statistical profile for each question, including factors like difficulty and discrimination. The GRE and GMAT utilize statistical profiling to assess their questions. The publishing methodology explains how various mathematical estimates come together to form a universal ranking system, including error bounds and normalization methods. The adaptive issue should be resolved through an independent audit by experts. Pilot results should be shared with broader stakeholders and vetted by them, especially regarding how scores on adaptive academic tests respond to system turbulence. More specifically, are they stable? Since the test is time-limited, there will be an error bound when students follow multiple trajectories to termination.

While the public debates IITs' foray into AI‑adaptive entrance testing, the judiciary must also be alert to the potential fallout from inaccuracies in SSIQ estimates. Adaptive tests inherently introduce estimation errors because candidates navigate uneven difficulty profiles until the test terminates. As a result, SSIQ may not yield a universal ranking scheme but instead cluster candidates into groups. This increases the likelihood of disputes within [JOSSA](https://josaa.nic.in/) and will necessitate the development and fine-tuning of a new seat allocation algorithm.

With AI-driven, adaptive JEE and institutional linkages like ALIGN, IITs are signaling a shift away from rigid exam bottlenecks. These initiatives mark a decisive shift in India's exam culture. Yet their credibility will rest not on design alone, but on whether they can withstand scrutiny for fairness, transparency, and resilience.
