## NEET Paper Leak 2026

[NTA decided to cancel the NEET 2026 exams following the allegations of a paper leak](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/education/news/nta-cancels-neet-ug-2026-examination-fresh-test-dates-to-be-announced-separately/articleshow/131031125.cms). The leak's origin has been traced to Nashik. The city is known for its wineries, such as Sula Vineyards, and is also a major Hindu pilgrimage site. Its Pandavleni Caves are 2000 years old and linked to the Pandavs of the Mahabharata. Unfortunately, the city is linked to two major scams, including the NEET 2026 paper leaks.'

The  NEET 2026 exam was conducted on 3rd May 2026, and the leak was discovered on 12th May. Out of 410 questions, 120 questions were exact matches with the paper of 3rd May. Investigators with the Rajasthan Police's Special Operations Group found the leak by reviewing 400 questions from a question bank circulated among candidates a few days before the examination. SOG subsequently raided multiple locations, including Sikar and Dehradun, to determine the extent of the leak. Many videos and copies of test papers linked to a coaching academy in Latur also surfaced. 

NTA was established in 2017. Its 9-year existence is riddled with ignominious paper leaks. A total of [70 paper leaks](https://www.indiatoday.in/education-today/featurephilia/story/paper-leaks-in-india-over-17-crore-aspirants-affected-in-7-years-2555716-2024-06-20)  at a rate of 10 per year occurred across 15 states over the last 7 years. It seriously undermines NTA's credibility. Before 2017, the national-level UG entrance tests were conducted by bodies such as AICTE and NCERT, or by academic institutions such as IITs. 

 In 2010, IIT directors recommended the creation of a central testing agency to relieve the burden of the entrance test, mainly at the persuasion of the MHRD. Kapil Sibal wanted to dismantle the IIT JEE and control IIT admissions through a centralized agency. However, IIT faculty opposed the MHRD and retained control over the advanced JEE. The responsibility of conducting JEE Mains was given to AICTE by MHRD. IITs were told to use JEE Main as a filter to select examinees eligible to appear for JEE Advanced. IITs were happy with the arrangement, as it reduced their burden for conducting a nationwide entrance examination. 

I am aware of two instances of JEE paper leaks before the transition to the two-tier JEE happened in 2013. Of course, mobile phones and internet access were limited before 2013. However, the commitment to IIT JEE was extraordinary and not found elsewhere. Being associated with IIT JEE, I am aware of the commitments of paper setters and exam supervisors. IITs recognized that candidates selected through JEE would be their students, and a strong sense of ownership existed. NTA employees work for a salary. There is no sense of ownership among them. So, like other government offices, NTA leaks and leaks very badly. Unfortunately, successive union governments view education as a non-priority sector in nation-building. Though the Education Minister holds cabinet rank, their job is more aligned with winning elections than with strengthening education institutions. From time to time, the ministry is embroiled in controversial decisions, such as the circulation of discriminatory caste-based regulations and the control of HEIs. 

Now, let us focus a bit on the consequences of paper leaks. The IIT JEE pattern changed from subjective to multiple-choice questions from 2006. From 1961 to 1999, IITs relied on the conventional long-question format. However, finding graders became increasingly difficult, and the use of graders outside the IIT system was unacceptable to the exam chairpersons. Therefore, IITs introduced a screening stage in 2000 to filter out candidates from the subjective test. The JEE screening test used a multiple-choice question format. However, the screening stage was discontinued from 2007; only a multiple-choice, machine-gradable paper pattern was introduced in the JEE exam. However, IIT did not rely on question banks; every time the question setters came up with a new paper. The questions were innovative and relied on a root question to test a candidate's depth of understanding of the concept and the subjects. 

The stakes in NEET are much higher compared to JEE. Therefore, it is not fair to compare the two different, unrelated exams. However, exam conduct should still be consistent to ensure confidentiality and sanctity. I don't know about NEET question setters. How they are picked, or how many of them take ownership of the paper. NEET also relies on creating question banks. I don't know whether the question bank is created each time or if it's a running bank. 

The government of India formed a high-powered committee, headed by K Radhakrishnan, to recommend reforms to the NEET exams following the 2024 paper leak. The committee strongly recommended a computer-based test (CBT). However, NTA cited a significant infrastructure deficit as a reason to continue the paper-based test. In two years, NTA could not build the infrastructure for CBT. CBT could be implemented effectively even without large-scale infrastructure through the following approach:
- Deploy an encrypted, low-power server at each exam center.
- Require candidates to use standardized, pre-verified laptops provided by or certified by NTA to ensure security. 
- The server would serve one questions at a time to the candidate's laptop and record the candidate's response before serving the next question.
- Additionally, server would record the entire logs of candidates' behavior during the exam.
  
I think TCS and several small IT companies have capabilities to create CBT within a year.  I am a bit surprised why the Radhakrishnan committee did not propose an implementable idea.

Frequent paper leaks raise serious doubts about whether each step in the process is formally validated a priori. It is difficult to pontificate from the outside unless we know the details. However, unless NTA conducts regular audits and holds accountability for every aspect of exam conduct, its credibility will take a tailspin under public scrutiny. Beyond punitive measures, NTA should establish systematic oversight by independent experts, vetting of question setters, and technological safeguards to ensure exam integrity.
