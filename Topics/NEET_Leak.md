## NEET Paper Leaks: Crisis of Exam Integrity and Beyond

[Blog Index](../index.md)

[Link to earlier post on NEET 2024](Neet_test2024.md)

[NTA decided to cancel the NEET 2026 exams following the allegations of a paper leak](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/education/news/nta-cancels-neet-ug-2026-examination-fresh-test-dates-to-be-announced-separately/articleshow/131031125.cms). The leak's origin has been traced to Nashik. The city is known for its wineries, such as Sula Vineyards, and is also a major Hindu pilgrimage site. Its Pandavleni Caves are 2000 years old and linked to the Pandavs of the Mahabharata. Unfortunately, the city is linked to two major scams, including the NEET 2026 paper leaks.

The  NEET 2026 exam was conducted on 3rd May 2026, and the leak was discovered on 12th May. 120 out of 410 questions were exact matches with the paper of 3rd May. Investigators with the Rajasthan Police's Special Operations Group (SOG) found the leak by reviewing a question bank circulated among candidates a few days before the examination. SOG subsequently raided multiple locations, including Sikar and Dehradun, to determine the extent of the leak. Many videos and copies of test papers linked to a coaching academy in Latur also surfaced. 


Kapil Sibal mooted the idea of [One Nation, One Test](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/iits-band-against-kapil-sibals-one-nation-one-test-487665) theory in 2012. Earlier in 2010, a group of IIT directors recommended the creation of the National Testing Agency (NTA) to relieve the burden of the entrance test. However, IIT faculty opposed the MHRD and retained control over the advanced JEE. The responsibility of conducting JEE Mains was given to AICTE by MHRD. IITs were told to use JEE Mains as a filter to select examinees eligible to appear for JEE Advanced. IITs were happy with the arrangement, as it substantially reduced the logistical burden for conducting a nationwide entrance examination. Only two instances of JEE paper leaks occurred before the transition to the two-tier JEE in 2013. Of course, mobile phones and internet access were limited before 2013. However, the level of commitment to IIT JEE from faculty and staff was extraordinary. They recognized that candidates selected through JEE would be their students, and a strong sense of ownership existed.

NTA was established in 2017. Its 9-year existence is riddled with ignominious paper leaks. A total of [70 paper leaks](https://www.indiatoday.in/education-today/featurephilia/story/paper-leaks-in-india-over-17-crore-aspirants-affected-in-7-years-2555716-2024-06-20)  at a rate of 10 per year occurred across 15 states over the last 7 years. It seriously undermines NTA's credibility. Before 2017, the national-level UG entrance tests were conducted by bodies such as AICTE and NCERT, or by academic institutions such as IITs. NTA employees work for a salary. There is no sense of ownership among them. So, like other government offices, NTA leaks and leaks very badly. Unfortunately, successive union governments view education as a non-priority sector in nation-building. Though the Education Minister holds cabinet rank, the ministers think their job is more aligned with winning elections than with strengthening educational institutions. From time to time, the ministry is embroiled in controversial decisions, such as the circulation of discriminatory caste-based regulations and the control of HEIs. 

Now, let us focus a bit on the consequences of paper leaks. The IIT JEE pattern changed from subjective to multiple-choice questions from 2006. From 1961 to 1999, IITs relied on the conventional long-question format. However, finding graders became increasingly difficult, and the use of graders outside the IIT system was unacceptable to the exam chairpersons as many discrepancies were reported by chief graders. Therefore, IITs introduced a screening stage in 2000 to filter out candidates from the subjective test. The JEE screening test used a multiple-choice question format. However, the screening stage was discontinued from 2007; only a multiple-choice, machine-gradable paper pattern was introduced in the JEE exam. However, IIT did not rely on question banks; every time the question setters came up with a new paper. The questions were innovative and relied on a root question to test a candidate's depth of understanding of the concept and the subjects. 

The stakes in NEET are much higher compared to JEE. Therefore, it is not fair to compare the two different, unrelated exams. However, exam conduct should still be consistent to ensure confidentiality and sanctity. I don't know about NEET question setters. How they are picked, or how many of them take ownership of the paper. NEET also relies on creating question banks. I don't know whether the question bank is created each time or if it's a running bank. 

The government of India formed a [high-powered committee, headed by K Radhakrishnan](./Nalanda_NTA.md), to recommend reforms to the NEET exams following the 2024 paper leak. The committee strongly recommended a computer-based test (CBT). However, NTA cited a significant infrastructure deficit as a reason to continue the paper-based test. In two years, NTA could not build the infrastructure for CBT. CBT could be implemented effectively even without a large-scale infrastructure through the following approach:
- Deploy an encrypted, low-power server at each exam center.
- Require candidates to use standardized, pre-verified laptops provided by or certified by NTA to ensure security. 
- The server would serve one question at a time to the candidate's laptop and record the candidate's response before serving the next question.
- Additionally, the server would record the entire logs of candidates' behavior during the exam.
  
TCS and several [small IT companies](https://blog.imocha.io/online-assessment-companies-in-india) have the capabilities to create CBT within a year. I am surprised that the Radhakrishnan committee did not propose an implementable idea.

Frequent paper leaks raise serious doubts about whether NTA has formally validated each step in the process. Most large-scale computer-assisted systems in India have faced similar challenges in the past. For example, the online ITR filing system in 2021 took nearly six months to stabilize after severe user feedback; GATE 2014, conducted in full CBT mode for the first time, encountered a few major issues; and Aadhaar required 7–8 years to reach stability. However, NEET UG handles about 2.5 million candidates — a scale that India's IT ecosystem can easily support. NTA could adopt a cautious two-tier CBT approach: 
- Use institutional labs where infrastructure exists, and
- Deploy laptop-based CBT elsewhere.

Unless NTA conducts regular audits and holds itself accountable for every aspect of exam conduct, its credibility will take a hit under public scrutiny. Beyond punitive measures, NTA should establish systematic oversight by independent experts, vetting of question setters, and technological safeguards to ensure exam integrity.  

<strong>Edits and updates:</strong>  [The education minister announced that the NEET exams will be conducted in CBT mode from 2027](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/neet-2027-to-be-held-in-computer-based-test-mode-education-min-dharmendra-pradhan-101778828363968.html). So, the question is why the exams were not conducted in CBT mode from 2026 when the minister also declared all recommendations of the Radhakrishnan committee have been implemented in toto. The masterminds behind large-scale leaks were among the [question setters](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/cbi-arrests-retired-professor-on-nta-panel-that-set-neet-paper/articleshow/131129801.cms). Some mid-level officers of NTA would have recommended these question setters. The NTA chairperson or higher echelons did not involve themselves. So, the ownership is absent.
