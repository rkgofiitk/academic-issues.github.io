## Ph.D. at IITs: Between Aspiration and Exploitation

[Blog Index](../index.md)

Behind the prestige of IITs lies a hidden struggle: the Ph.D. journey, often romanticized as the pinnacle of academic pursuit, can in reality feel like a test of endurance against systemic neglect and supervisory exploitation. [A recent news item on the travails and tribulations of doing a Ph.D. at IIT Kanpur](https://www.reddit.com/r/IITK/comments/1vpoo36/exploited_and_broke_the_reality_of_doing_a_phd_at/?solution=47279d50e75f5d9e47279d50e75f5d9e&js_challenge=1&token=7afd7253fec22262ff1c52b1703fe9ecbaf932f35fcdac3bf3f8744c1ac22734&jsc_orig_r=&utm_source=copilot.com) describes the grind of working 9-to-9, six days a week. Even with a weekly day off, many supervisors treat Ph.D. students as bonded labor until graduation. This is not unique to IITK—it reflects a global pattern. Accounts of exploitation surface even from prestigious universities abroad. The problem is particularly acute when students are tied to a professor’s research grants as Research Assistants (RAs). In contrast, securing a Teaching Assistantship (TA) often offers greater independence, especially at U.S. universities, where financial survival is otherwise precarious. Not for nothing was the satirical film [Ph.D. (Piled Higher and Deeper)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uMFKu8ies0c&utm_source=copilot.com) made—it captures a universal reality.

### Choosing the Supervisor: A Critical Decision

At IITs, the choice of supervisor can determine whether a student’s years are invested wisely or wasted. Due diligence is essential. A few guiding steps:
Talk to seniors.
- Review social media reputations.
- Balance interpersonal skills with academic rigor.
- Investigate grant history alongside student outcomes.
  
Some supervisors may appear welcoming—offering unsolicited help or invitations—but lack academic depth. Others may be academically strong yet indifferent to mentoring. Students must build a profile of prospective supervisors before committing.

### The Difficulty of Changing Supervisors

Formally, IITs allow students to change supervisors. In practice, the scope is limited and fraught with obstacles. Institutes often discourage it, and if the supervisor is vindictive or uncooperative, the option may be impossible. Departments resist students moving outside their boundaries, fearing reputational damage. Interdisciplinary research is feasible only if co-supervisors are registered from the outset and backed by substantial joint grants. Otherwise, as numerous Reddit posts attest, changing supervisors is a bureaucratic maze that few successfully navigate.

### Supervisors and Career Advancement

Another dimension of exploitation lies in how supervisors showcase student achievements for their own career advancement:
- Counting Ph. D.S graduated.
- Highlighting grant volumes.
- Accumulating research awards.
  
There are even cases where supervisors have received higher doctorates (D.Sc.) based largely on student work. Universities rarely scrutinize such practices. Worse, misconduct—ranging from violations of Vishaka guidelines to caste-based abuse—often goes unchecked unless formally reported. Students must remain vigilant, as institutions seldom act proactively.

### The Data Behind the Struggles

Without statistics, such warnings may sound like unsolicited advice. Yet the numbers are sobering. IIT Kanpur has a postgraduate dropout rate of 17.5%. [Government responses attribute the withdrawals to job offers](https://www.facebook.com/groups/1015191011986167/posts/1234571456714787/?utm_source=copilot.com), but [independent surveys tell a different story](https://voxiitk.com/piled-higher-and-deeper-phd-iitk-i/?utm_source=copilot.com). A student-led survey found the average time to completion is seven years—far too long for students entering at 23–24. [Another survey revealed that 60% of Ph.D. students contemplated dropping out, while 80% struggled with mental health and stress](https://www.indiatoday.in/education-today/news/story/80-iit-kanpur-phd-scholars-struggle-with-mental-health-policy-change-coming-2497078-2024-02-03?utm_source=copilot.com).

### Conclusion and Call to Action

Ultimately, the Ph.D. journey is shaped less by institutional rules than by the supervisor-student dynamic. Vigilance, due diligence, and collective awareness are the only safeguards against exploitation. But individual caution is not enough. Unless institutions confront the supervisor-student power imbalance head-on, reforms will remain cosmetic, and students will continue to bear the brunt of systemic neglect. IITs—and universities more broadly—must establish independent grievance mechanisms, enforce accountability for misconduct, and empower student unions to represent doctoral scholars. Only then can the Ph.D. be reclaimed as a path of intellectual growth rather than a prolonged struggle for survival.
