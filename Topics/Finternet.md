

## Finternet: Unified Tokenized Financial Infrastructure

[Blog Index](../index.md)

In casual conversations in Hindi, people use words with other similar-sounding words in rhyme to semantically dilute or trivialize the seriousness of a statement. For example, "roti-shoti", "kam-sham", "padhai-vadhai", "techie-shachie", etc. The examples "rhyme-based [neologism](https://literaryterms.net/neologism/)" are drawn from the dialects of central UP, particularly Kanpur, where I had spent the better part of my time. It is a pleasant surprise when similar rhyming words with "Internet" have been created for technical terminology. 

We do come across descriptions of recursive terminology like 
- "meta-model": Describes the structure of other models in software engineering
- "meta-meta-model: Used for modeling of a meta-model
- "type-type": A type whose values are types.
- "compiler-compiler": A compiler for generating a compiler
- "policy-policy": Rules for creating policies in governance
- "control-control": Controlling mechanism governing the controls.

However, recursive terminology is not equivalent to  "rhyme-based neologism" used in casual conversations on the streets of UP or elsewhere in India. In English, "semantic bleaching" refers to the process of making a concept or idea more forceful or explicit. For example, euphemistically we may use "AI echics" and "Digital inclusive" to mean the same thing. The clear etymological distinction between Anglo-Saxon and Oriental languages may deceive the reader about the topic of this post. So, let me pull back to the subject of discussion. Although mentioning "Finternet" alongside "Internet" may seem like an example of rhyme-based neologism to some, the term actually has a deep technical meaning. 

[Finternet](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1178.pdf) aims to create an innovative, unified, robust, and trusted framework for trade and commerce, financial services, wealth and investment management, lending and credit services, cryptocurrency, stable currencies, micro payments, digital wallets, and regulatory compliance.  Finternet will revolutionize the global financial ecosystem by networking tokenized fintech data and analytic services. The idea is potentially more impactful than [Satoshi Nakamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satoshi_Nakamoto)'s peer-to-peer (P2P) cryptocurrency exchange system. However, its proposed transparent system of converting a multi-dimensional fintech problem into a unidimensional tokenized substitute could create serious issues with the political will to implement it. Let us examine why.

Since the 17th century, Britain has been one of the largest consumers of Tea. Dutch and Portuguese traders introduced [Tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea) to Europe as an expensive luxury item among the elites in social circles. China was very secretive in sharing tea plants with foreign traders. It only accepted silver in exchange for Tea. [The British East India Company's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) agents stole tea plants from China to cultivate in India, thereby gaining a monopoly on tea distribution across Europe. Until the company gained expertise in cultivating Tea in India and British colonies, it continued to engage in illegal opium trading in exchange for Tea.  The East India Company became the British Monarch's only accredited drug pusher after the [Opium War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Opium_War).  Many historians attribute the Opium War to one of the main reasons behind the Tea Trade. However, the fact is that the East  India Company's greed had no bounds. They wanted to sell Opium and have a monopoly on Tea as well.  Today's world is marked by numerous layers of complexity, as exemplified by the trade of Tea and Opium. Global trade is not determined  by transparency alone, but by many factors:
- Regulatory and Policy framework
- Logistical supply chain and infrastructure
- Environmental sustainability standards
- Technological integration
- Statistical and analytical parameters
- Politico-religious-cultural factors

The "coincidence of double needs" determines exchange value..  The lack of standardization breaks down the trade. In global trade, the "Euro-Dollar" plays a pivotal role not because it has a stable exchange rate, but because the US and European nations back it, and it facilitates a swift inter-banking digital exchange system.   

The idea of digitally converting all financial assets, such as gold, silver, properties, securities, bank deposits, and contracts, into interoperable tokens will have even fewer takers in India. India is a noisy democracy where the country is perpetually in electioneering mode. Election is not state-funded. Political survivability is based on creating fragmentation along multiple fault lines that require massive illegal funding. It is possible only through illicit deals and transactions. Bringing transparency to property deals is like intercepting a falcon mid-dive with bare hands. Approximately 60% of civil litigations in India involve property disputes and financial transactions. More than 18k district and subordinate courts are engaged in such disputes, devoting 25-30% of their time to them. About 1.4 million lawyers are also involved. Any attempt to bring transparency to the domain of the legal ecosystem and courts could create a significant upheaval and be fiercely resisted.
