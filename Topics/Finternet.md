## Finternet: Unified Tokenized Financial Infrastructure

In casual conversation in Hindi, people use words with other similar-sounding words in rhyme to semantically dilute or trivialize the seriousness of a statement. For example, "roti-shoti", "kam-sham", "padhai-vadhai", "techie-shachie", etc. The examples are drawn from the dialect of central UP, particularly Kanpur, where I had spent the better part of my time. It is a pleasant surprise when similar rhyming words with "Internet" have been created for technical terminology. We do come across descriptions of recursive terminology like 
- "meta-model": Describes the structure of other models in software engineering
- "meta-meta-model: Used for modeling of a meta-model
- "type-type": A type whose values are types.
- "compiler-compiler": A compiler for generating a compiler
- "policy-policy": Rules for creating policies in governance
- "control-control": Controlling mechanism governing the controls.

However, recursion is not exactly a "rhyme-based neologism" as mentioned in casual conversation on the streets of UP or elsewhere in India. In English, "semantic bleaching" refers to the process of making a concept or idea more forceful or explicit. For example, euphemistically we may use "AI echics" and "Digital inclusive" to mean the same thing. The clear etymological distinction between Anglo-Saxon and Oriental languages may deceive the reader about the topic of this post. So, let me pull back to the subject of discussion. Although mentioning "Finternet" alongside "Internet" may seem like an example of rhyme-based neologism to some, "Finternet" is actually a term with a deep technical meaning. 

Finternet represents a unified, tokenized financial infrastructure. The idea is to digitally convert all financial assets, such as gold, silver, properties, securities, bank deposits, and contracts, into interoperable tokens.
