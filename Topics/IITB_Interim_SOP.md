## Fitting Socrates to Exams in the Age of AI

### Why IIT Bombay's Interim SOP Misses the Big Picture? 

[Blog Index](../index.md)

IIT Bombay's Academic Senate has created a [new interim Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/mumbai/no-frisking-fresh-answer-book-if-caught-cheating-bags-allowed-in-exam-building-iit-bombays-new-interim-sop-for-october-10-11-tests/articleshow/134777998.cms) for the conduct of examinations. The major changes are:

- Security guards will not frisk students before they enter the exam hall.  
- Students are allowed to bring their bags into the building but must leave them outside the exam hall.  
- If an invigilator suspects a student of cheating, they will record the incident, call the counselors, and request that the student empty their pockets in front of the counselors.  
- If cheating is confirmed, the invigilators will notify the Quick Response Team to follow the student to the hostel after they leave the exam hall.
  
The automatic semester suspension is kept on hold for the time being. The first offense will not result in a semester drop; repeat offenders may receive the punishment. Whether the interim SOP becomes the contours of a permanent SOP remains to be seen.  

The interim SOP recognizes that physical frisking entails an intrusion into a student's privacy. Allowing students to carry bags into the building and requiring them to leave them outside the exam hall may seem like a pragmatic exam policing policy. However,  it does not grapple with sophisticated AI-enabled cheating. 

Mobile phones are relatively easy to detect, but cloud-supported wearable devices are not. A resourceful student can enter the exam hall wearing an AI-enabled pair of glasses linked via Bluetooth to a phone kept in a bag outside. They could take a picture of the question paper and silently upload it to the cloud using a specially designed script that transmits answers in real time. Ignoring this possibility means conceding defeat before the exam even begins. The Senate's focus on eliminating frisking or allowing bags fails to recognize that technology can easily outsmart standard exam security measures.

The unusual reactive-mode firefighting that prompted IIT Bombay's Academic Senate should serve as a wake-up call for all educators and society as a whole. A broader point is that these reactive measures cannot effectively address the fundamental problems with current invigilation practices. Exams serve as a means of assessing learning, mental maturity, and problem-solving capabilities. Therefore, it is not the design of the exams that matters as much as whether they fulfill these objectives. Reactive approaches to exam policing fall short of achieving these goals, as the conflict between policing and cheating has evolved. The core issue lies in eliminating the structural flaws within the existing exam system.

The failure to anticipate technological change often mirrors a larger pattern of poor governance: reactive to crises shaped by flashpoints rather than foresight. Just as streets and mobs cannot adjudicate misgovernance, reactive SOPs cannot resolve the structural flaws in our examination system. Stricter surveillance is not the solution,  but innovation in testing mechanisms is. The Academic Senate of IIT Bombay should have mulled over an innovative framework for the examination system and student evaluation. The safeguards on which the current exam and evaluation system is designed are obsolete and have been rendered ineffective by technological advancements. Therefore, the only sustainable response will be to evolve the exam itself. 

Many institutions have experimented with alternative, effective evaluation mechanisms that practically shun conventional exams. A few of these are:

- Project-based testing requires students to apply formulas, theories, and engineering design principles to specific use cases based on the topics.    
- Open resources/book exams: Allow students to use the Internet, AI tools, and books to solve the question paper. The question paper design is a key issue here. Ensure that resource help is minimized. 
- Viva: Interview students individually or in groups to assess the depth of their understanding.
- Peer evaluation: Students evaluate their peers on seminars and project presentations.
  
These methods do not eliminate cheating; they render it irrelevant by shifting the focus from recall to reasoning. The instructors can calibrate the evaluation mechanisms by a judicious combination of these methods. They may inject a surprise element from time to time to challenge a student's intellectual maturity in applying the subject knowledge. 

The conduct of examinations should be based on the realities of our times. If IITs or other institutions of national importance want to stay ahead, they must lead in creative testing mechanisms rather than the old exam system. Otherwise, technology will continue to expose the cracks in a system built for the pre-electronic era.
