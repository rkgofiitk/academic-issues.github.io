## Satya Niketan Collapse: Unsafe Housing and a Broken University System

[Blog Index](../index.md)

On 6th September, a terrible mishap occurred near the Delhi University South Campus, when seven students were buried under the rubble of Paying Guest (PG)  accommodation in the Satya Niketan building. The rescue team pulled out twelve students, and five were injured. The Delhi government has since ordered a safety audit, while the Delhi High Court has initiated a citywide inspection of paying guest (PG) accommodations. It raises a serious question: why do the authorities act after a tragedy?

The central and state governments in India always adopt a checkbox-type formulaic approach to deal with complex issues. Preventive measures are delayed until disasters force the authorities to act.  Politicians and the bureaucracy thrive in this ecosystem, conserving resources and waiting for negligence to become fatal. In this mixed calculus, a human life is a mere statistic that can be compensated for with a few lacs. The Satya Niketan collapse is not just a case of unsafe housing, but reflects a deeper neglect of student welfare within the university system itself.

Students are undone not only by the frequent structural and unlivable conditions in PG accommodations but also by a broken University system.
Other troubling effects include:
- Curricula design encourages rote learning and mediocrity, not creativity.
- Widespread quota systems block meritorious students from technical programs, thereby lowering the level of competence in technical skill development.  
- Faculty recruitment is skewed towards non-essential subjects with little scope for modernization. 
- Degrees fail to secure employment, leaving graduates unproductive, unskilled, and idle. 

It leads to a nation full of non-performers who depend on the government welfare measures. It drills a continuously sinking hole without contributing to the country's GDP. Interestingly, India has slipped off its trajectory toward becoming the 3rd-largest economy in global rankings, even as leaders continue to promise the cherished place. 

While the broken university system is part of a larger debate, we can quickly fix the problem of PG accommodation disasters with a bit of decent planning. The universities, in collaboration with the government, can develop a layered policy-recommendation framework for the off-campus housing system. 
- The government and local authorities (DDA & MCD) designate student zones.
- All unauthorized structures in student zones should be demolished and rebuilt in accordance with approved plans.
- Universities issue housing approval guidelines.
- The student groups with university oversight set the pricing and safety guidelines.
  
The layered recommendation structure won't work unless the university takes fiduciary responsibility for off-campus housing approval. No student should be admitted to any of the university's academic programs without guaranteed safe housing, either on-campus or off-campus. I hope the government listens.
