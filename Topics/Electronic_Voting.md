## Electronic Voting System & Voter's Identity 

[Blog Index](../index.md)

ECI conducted the SIR (Special Intensive Revisions) of electoral rolls across 12 states and successfully conducted the election process in 4 states: Assam, West Bengal, Tamil Nadu, and Puducherry. It is a commendable, yet most thankless, task in India. Conducting an incident-free election in West Bengal is indeed an outstanding job. However, our discussion here is not only about ensuring a violence-free election, but also about preserving the sanctity and the legitimacy of a free and fair election with minimal deployment of security forces and human resources.

Let us look back to trace the origins of the concepts of election and voting, and to understand why elections and voting remain a time-tested means of acquiring legitimacy to govern. The election dates back to 508 BC, and most historians think it originated in Ancient Greece. The etymological origin of the word "election" is linked to the Greek word "eklektos." It means choosing, picking, or selecting from a set of options. The process of choosing is known as voting. The older Greek method of election relied on a show of hands. In Ancient India, the evidence of [non-monarchial governments](https://www.infinityfoundation.com/mandala/h_es/h_es_muhlb_democra_frameset.htm) goes back to Vedic times. Republicanism in India was practiced vigorously during the fifth and sixth centuries BC, when the civilization progressively moved towards providing civic amenities and urbanization for the comforts of living. The earliest Buddhist scripture, "[Pali Canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pali_Canon)", gave a sufficiently detailed description of Vaisali as an urban settlement of parks, ponds, and planned buildings for human settlements. Panini's work [Astadhyayi](https://ashtadhyayi.com/), Mahabharata, [Jain Cannon](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Jaina-canon), and [Katuliya's Arthasasthra](https://archive.org/details/in.gov.ignca.900) dealt with ancient republicanism in India. The [Pandyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandya_dynasty), the [Pallavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallava_dynasty), the [Cholas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_dynasty), and the [Vijaynagar kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayanagara_Empire) had [the practice of seeking collective opinions from Sabhas](https://www.infinityfoundation.com/mandala/h_es/h_es_muhlb_democra_frameset.htm) in resolving conflicting issues and making administrative policy decisions. The Sabhas of ancient kingdoms were chosen from village communities. So, while voting and elections were largely well-established means of reaching consensus that affected large populations. 

The modern method of electronic electioneering in India did not fully evolve until the 1980s. The [first general elections in India were held in 1951-52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951%E2%80%9352). The futility of ballot paper-based election became transparent to citizens when large-scale [scientific rigging](https://grokipedia.com/page/scientific_rigging) became a reality during the period 1970 to 1990. Rigging defeats the very purpose of elections and robs citizens of their basic rights. There were many forms of election rigging, such as 
- Booth capturing
- Intimidation of election officials
- Source jamming
- Slow counting and tampering of tabulations
- Physical theft of ballot boxes
- Invalid votes
- Casting votes of absent and dead voters

Political parties organized local muscle men to orchestrate election rigging. The basic approach was to scare away the genuine voters and cast votes on their behalf. UP, Bihar, and West Bengal gained notoriety in gaming elections. As we can find, most of these rigging methods work even with electronic voting machines (EVMs). 

[Electronic Voting Machines were introduced in a phased manner from 1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_voting_in_India). Before the introduction of EVMs, ballot papers were used in Indian elections, as they still are in the USA and many other supposedly advanced nations. EVMs are simple, standalone, tamper-proof electronic voting machines that tally votes cast by voters. It eliminated only three things, viz., invalid votes, slow counting, and physical theft of ballots. However, the rest of the rigging techniques largely remained intact. Political patronage to organized goons and hooliganism became an accepted norm across parties that swear by the constitution! An echo system of political activism in the grip of a cadre of muscle men flourishes today. Political Action Companies sprang up to organize the process of rigging EVMs with assistance from Polling Officers, Police, and Oversight teams. However, the compliance mechanism for poll manipulation even through Political Action Companies relies on coercion and bribes.  The price of non-compliance could be as high as the loss of individual lives. So, ECI has to rely on a large posse of security forces to ward off threats from large-scale election-related violence across India. In the recently concluded elections of four states, ECI adopted several strong measures to ensure free and fair elections. In West Bengal alone, the ECI had to deploy over a quarter of a million security and central paramilitary forces to earn voters' confidence in running a violence-free election.

ECI has provisions for regularly updating voter rolls. However, the voter rolls were only for adding new voters. In 2025, just before the Bihar elections, the ECI conducted a Special Intensive Revision of the voter list. The last [Intensive Revision of Voters Rolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Intensive_Revision) was conducted in 2002. There is a 23-year gap between the two SIRs. ECI relied on AI and other modern software to flag many logical discrepancies. The political parties, rather than assisting the ECI, went to the Supreme Court to delay and derail the SIR process. Since the stakes in elections are very high, the ECI needs to conduct SIR every 5 years. The sanctity of the voters' list remains a hotly contested subject. The crux of the contest lies in the authenticity of voters and the ability to cast votes in a violence-free, fair atmosphere. How to ensure it?

Voters' identity and voting cannot be more important than ensuring the authenticity of financial transactions, property deals, secured entries into airports, defense, and other sensitive establishments. We have a well-established biometrics-based robust protocol for the latter. Why can't voting and elections be turned into an entirely AI-based automated process? Let us analyze the proposal a bit in depth. 

[Billions of digital payments are made every day](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/upi-achieves-22-35-bn-transactions-in-april-dfs/130814926?utm_source=copilot.com). As of early 2026, 660 million UPI transactions are processed daily. The value of these transactions tops 10 billion US dollars a day. Besides, UPI and other digital modes such as NEFT, IMPS, RTGS, and FAST Tag average over 12 million transactions per day. RBI reports an average of Rs. 1.40 per hundred thousand rupees is disputed or classified as fraudulent transactions. Total loss is estimated at 2.5 billion rupees (approximately 261 million US dollars) annually, a substantial amount. Detecting cybercriminals remains a challenge, but the efficiency and convenience of the digital payment system outweigh the losses. Counterfeiting and untraceable fraudulent transactions were major concerns in the physical payment system that preceded the digital payment system. Though it is difficult to give a proper estimate due to untraceable transactions, trillions of dollars are lost in tax revenue, savings of 520 million USD in currency management, and a shadow economy worth nearly a quarter of India's total productivity, severely weakening the Indian economy.

Let us now examine the impacts of [biometric-based authentication of property rights](https://legal-veda.com/biometric-data-privacy-laws-in-india/?utm_source=copilot.com), which is now mandatory across India. It has unlocked about 2% of frozen land assets, thereby contributing to the country's overall GDP. It has made benami transactions almost impossible and has eliminated the menace of nexus between politicians and land mafia. Furthermore, the process empowered small landowners to claim settlement of compensation when the government acquires land for infrastructure development or large CAP projects. Blockchain-based property management has also reduced litigation, enabling faster, more efficient access to loans and mortgages. Coupled with a smart contract, the frozen land assets become a growth engine. In fact, property-related litigations account for 66% of civil cases across Indian courts. 

An AI-driven system backed by biometric authentication has served well in finance, banking, insurance, stock markets, property dealings, and surveillance-based secured entry to sensitive places, including airports. The secured electronic ecosystem has deservedly earned people's faith. Since elections underpin the very foundation of democratic legitimacy, they deserve a trusted, time-tested foundation of authenticity. Why can't the election and voting process be elevated to the next level of electronic upgradation? Voters can cast and authenticate their votes using [hand-held devices, ATMS, or upgraded EVMs](https://www.medianama.com/2026/04/223-supreme-court-pil-notice-biometric-voter-verification/?utm_source=copilot.com). Taking disputed transaction data into account, it will be possible to limit the number of spurious votes to no more than 2 per 100,000 voters. A quick calculation based on the data shows that, for a state like West Bengal, with just over 65 million voters, the number of disputed votes will not exceed 1,500! The requirements of election officials can be reduced, and the deployment of security forces can be substantially reduced. I am not saying that it will eliminate the role of muscle men in the election. But they will find it harder to rig the system. Any electronically secured system can be hacked, but it takes time and effort to engineer one. Introduce a surprise element every time to outsmart the hacking. The election will be much cleaner, securing not just voters' rights but also inclusivity and legitimacy of governance.
