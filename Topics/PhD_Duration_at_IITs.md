## PhDs at IITs: Caps Without Accountability

This post is a follow‑up to my earlier discussion of the challenges faced by PhD students at IITs. When ToI announced IIT Bombay’s plan to cap PhDs at eight years, it sounded like reform. In reality, IITs already enforce such limits—at IIT Kanpur, the maximum duration is six years, extendable to seven under special circumstances [(IIT Kanpur PG Manual, 2023‑24)](https://www.iitk.ac.in/mse/phd?utm_source=copilot.com). IIT Bombay’s own PhD rulebook specifies a six‑year validity period, with tightly regulated extensions [(IIT Bombay PhD Rules, Rule 8.5)](https://ganit.math.iitb.ac.in/Academics/PG_Rules.pdf?utm_source=copilot.com). The recent announcement [(CNBCTV18, Aug 2026)](https://www.cnbctv18.com/education/iit-bombay-caps-phd-duration-at-8-years-for-new-registrations-19975699.htm?utm_source=copilot.com), therefore, appears less a reform than a restatement of existing policy.

The Academic Senate must deliberate, and in most cases, only a one‑year extension is approved if both supervisor and student provide compelling reasons. In exceptional circumstances, if the Senate meeting is not scheduled before the expiry of the registration, the Senate Subcommittee on Post-Graduate Studies (SPGC) may grant further extensions. Still, these are strictly temporary—lasting no more than 6 months to 1 year—and require the Director's approval. The SPGC must also report such cases to the Senate.

Against this backdrop, the ToI news item is puzzling. IIT Bombay is reinventing something already codified in the postgraduate rules and procedures booklet.

### Why Limits Matter?

There are sound reasons for imposing a reasonable time limit on doctoral programs:
#### Academic considerations
- Six years is generally adequate for completing a PhD.
- Extending beyond eight often requires redefining the research problem, which in turn leads to further delays.
- Some candidates prolong their stay while pursuing other vocations or indulging in excessive extracurricular activities, even preparing for the civil services.

#### Institutional considerations
- Seniors overstaying in hostels strain the accommodation available to freshers.
- Lab resources remain tied up with long‑tenured students, limiting access for newer cohorts.

### A Case Study from the 1980s

One IIT (not from IITK) student I knew submitted a thesis before the six‑year limit. The external examiner sat on it for over a year, then raised objections that the research field was obsolete. Had the examiner rejected the thesis outright, the student could have approached another examiner from the panel. But objections trigger a different process: the candidate must rebut, revise, and resubmit. This creates a delay loop.
In this case, the student lacked suitable rebuttals and eventually lost patience. It was the early 1980s, when even email was not widely available at IITs. Such situations are unlikely today, with institutes and candidates integrated into the global research ecosystem and connected by high‑speed Internet.

### Institutional Bottlenecks

Delays are not always student‑driven. On the institute’s side, PhD backlogs accumulate for reasons such as:
- Supervisors are unavailable for extended periods, whether on sabbatical or tied up with projects.
- Students struggle to revise drafts to address supervisors’ objections substantively.
- External examiners declining or failing to take up assignments.
- Examiners sitting on theses for unexplained reasons after accepting invitations.
- Academic offices fail to send periodic reminders, despite rules requiring them to do so.
- Candidate rebuttals under exceptional conditions are adding further delay.
  
### The Real Reform

Thus, capping duration is not reform. Real reform lies in addressing supervisor‑student power dynamics and institutional accountability. Rules already exist; what matters is how they are enforced. An unwilling student can silently bring disrepute, while unchecked examiner delays or absent supervisors trap candidates in bureaucratic limbo. Unless institutions confront these structural issues head‑on, duration caps remain cosmetic. The real challenge is ensuring that PhD students are not left stranded between procedural rules and interpersonal bottlenecks. Only then will doctoral programs at IITs deliver on their promise without becoming endurance tests.
